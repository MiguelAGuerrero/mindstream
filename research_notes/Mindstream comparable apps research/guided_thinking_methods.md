# Guided thinking methods, prompt-sequenced tools, and reasoning/decision tools as comparables for Mindstream workflows

Method note for the report writer: findings below come from web-search result summaries gathered on 2026-11-24 (22 tool calls). Pages were not individually fetched and read in full, so each cited finding is "as reported by the search summary of that source". Anything I know only from prior knowledge and did not verify this session is placed under Gaps and labelled "unverified background". Do not present Gaps items as confirmed facts.

## 1. Prompt-guided thinking and journaling apps: how do they sequence prompts, allow user-authored prompt sets, and store results?

### Takeaway
Journaling and CBT apps are the closest consumer precedent for "an ordered list of prompts producing one entry", and at least Stoic and Day One let users author their own prompt sets; but none found treats a run as resolving to a typed outcome (call versus sharper question), nests one prompt set inside a step of another, or writes a result back to the text that triggered it. The strongest structural precedent in this group is the CBT thought record: a fixed 5 to 7 step sequence with a before/after rating that ends in exactly one "balanced thought".

### Cited Findings
- Stoic (iOS "stoic. journal & mental health") release notes describe "custom journal templates": users create their own journals, add any prompts or metrics, and save them as guided journals. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)
- Stoic's listing says core journaling is free while guided journals and reflections require premium; a later release note says AI prompts now adapt to the category being written in and the time of day (AI-dependent feature). — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)
- Stoic user complaint in reviews: prompts sometimes re-appear for the same category (work, fitness), i.e. repetition fatigue. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)
- Stoic's App Store version history reaches a 2026.x version number, indicating active maintenance in 2026. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)
- A separate app, "Stoicism - Praxis", offers structured prompt sets by method: morning planning, evening review, gratitude practice and fear-setting, plus AI "journal analysis" (AI-dependent). — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6738201959)
- Day One separates two features: (a) a template gallery plus user-made custom templates ("My templates" tab on web; on Android "New Template" with sections, text or lists), and (b) Daily Prompts, a curated prompt that rotates every 24 hours and creates a new entry when tapped. — [Day One templates guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/tips-and-tutorials/templates/); [Day One journal templates feature page](https://dayoneapp.com/features/journal-templates/); [Day One blog, "It's time for templates"](https://dayoneapp.com/blog/its-time-for-templates)
- Day One templates can be assigned to custom Reminders so a template becomes a recurring routine, and a default template can be assigned per journal (iOS, macOS, web only). — [Day One journal templates feature page](https://dayoneapp.com/features/journal-templates/); [Day One templates guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/tips-and-tutorials/templates/)
- Day One web custom templates may require the user's encryption key first (end-to-end encrypted cloud sync model, not local-first). — [Day One blog: new journal templates for Day One web](https://dayoneapp.com/blog/new-journal-templates-for-day-one-web/)
- Rosebud (journal app) is described in a 2026 review as "a conversational AI journal, not a prompt selector, but an actual dialogue"; the same review characterises it as higher friction, deeper engagement, more expensive, and dependent on premium for meaningful features. Guidance is AI-generated follow-up questions, so it is not transferable to Mindstream as-is. — [Macaron review, 2026](https://macaron.im/he/blog/rosebud-ai-journaling-app-review-2026)
- Rosebud also produces AI weekly summaries of patterns and suggests goals from entries (AI-dependent). A Trustpilot reviewer praises that it "automatically gives you prompts so you never get bored". One listing gives paid plans from $12.99/month with a free tier (not confirmed against the official site; many search results confuse it with an unrelated "Rosebud AI" game-maker). — [Trustpilot](https://at.trustpilot.com/review/rosebud.app); [GoodFirms listing](https://www.goodfirms.co/software/rosebud-ai)
- CBT thought record, canonical sequence: NHS presents it as "a set of 7 prompts" to capture and examine thoughts, feelings and the evidence for them. — [NHS Every Mind Matters: thought record](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/thought-record)
- Psychology Tools describes the logic: identify key negative automatic thoughts and the evidence that seems to support them, then re-evaluate by recording contradictory evidence, ending in an alternative/balanced thought (Padesky's version replaced "rational response" with "alternative/balanced thought"). — [Psychology Tools: thought records](https://www.psychologytools.com/self-help/thought-records); [Psychology Tools: 10 things therapists need to know](https://www.psychologytools.com/articles/thought-records-in-cbt-10-things-therapists-need-to-know)
- Guidance in the same literature: work on one strong thought at a time; a balanced thought need not remove the feeling entirely (e.g. anxiety from 80 to 50, not to zero). — [Psychology Tools: thought records](https://www.psychologytools.com/self-help/thought-records)
- App implementations: "Balm: CBT Thought Diary" (iPhone) walks situation, emotion (rated 0 to 100), automatic thought (with belief rating), rational response, and "gently reminds you about open records" (an unfinished-run reminder). — [App Store: Balm](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789923194)
- "Thought Tracker CBT" captures situation, automatic thought, emotion, evidence, alternative view, and outcome in a simple step-by-step flow. — [AlternativeTo: Thought Tracker CBT](https://alternativeto.net/software/thought-tracker-cbt/about/)
- "Thought Diary" (iOS) begins by asking what you were doing and where you were. "Pocket CBT" (iOS) has two main steps; reviewers liked that it is evidence-based but could not locate a privacy policy. — [App Store: Thought Diary](https://apps.apple.com/za/app/thought-diary/id385433207); [University at Buffalo review of Pocket CBT](https://ubwp.buffalo.edu/happe/2019/01/09/pocket-cbt/)
- Woebot is discontinued: the consumer app was retired on 30 June 2025 (announced by email in late April 2025; users could export conversation history until the last day). Founder Alison Darcy attributed the decision largely to the cost and difficulty of meeting FDA requirements. — [STAT News, 2 July 2025](https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/); [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/woebot-health-shutting-down-its-app); [HLTH](https://hlth.com/insights/news/woebot-health-is-shutting-down-its-app-2025-04-28)

### Inferences
- Two authoring models exist: "template" (all prompts laid out at once as headings in one entry: Day One) and "guided journal" (prompts presented in sequence: Stoic, CBT apps). Mindstream workflows are the second kind; the Day One model is the fallback rendering of a finished run (a single document with the prompts as headings).
- Borrowable mechanics: (1) CBT's before/after rating bracket (rate belief in the claim 0 to 100 before the run and after) gives a cheap numeric trace of whether a "Doubt" run moved anything, and feeds deliberate-practice comparison later; (2) CBT's "one hot thought at a time" rule matches Mindstream's "each step resolves into exactly one statement"; (3) CBT's permitted partial outcome (80 to 50, not zero) is a precedent for "a sharper question" being a legitimate ending; (4) Balm's reminder about open records is a light version of the open-item gate; (5) Day One's template-to-reminder binding suggests letting a workflow variant be bound to a trigger or context.
- Reported complaints that map to Mindstream risks: prompt repetition fatigue (Stoic), and depth gated behind premium/AI (Rosebud). A user-owned library with variants directly answers the repetition complaint.
- No journaling app found has nested prompt sets, typed step outputs (text versus action), or write-back to an originating passage. I found no comparable feature for these three.

### Gaps
- Reflectly, Journey, Clearful, Jour, Reflection.app, The Five Minute Journal app, Daylio, MoodTools and "CBT Thought Record Diary" were not individually verified this session (searches returned nothing usable). Unverified background: Reflection.app markets "guides" (multi-prompt guided journals) and is cloud-based; Five Minute Journal is a fixed morning/evening prompt pair; Daylio is mood/activity taps with optional notes and custom "goals", not sequenced prompts; Jour was reported discontinued some years ago; Reflectly is AI-flavoured mood journaling. None of these should be stated as fact without checking.
- Current pricing for Stoic, Day One, Rosebud (official figures) and exact platform coverage in late 2026 not confirmed.
- How each app stores results (export format, per-prompt fields versus a single text blob) was not confirmed from documentation.
- No user-sentiment data found specifically on user-authored prompt sets (do people actually build and reuse them?).

## 2. Templates and repeatable procedures inside tools for thought: how far toward guided step-by-step runs with a result?

### Takeaway
Tools for thought mostly stop at "template expansion with input prompts": Roam SmartBlocks, Obsidian QuickAdd/Templater and Tana command nodes can chain steps and ask for inputs, but they are scripted macros that fire and finish; none found models a run as a persistent object with a current step, an outcome type, or a gate. This is the clearest white space for Mindstream.

### Cited Findings
- Roam SmartBlocks: "workflows" are the definitions of what a SmartBlock does; blocks can contain Commands (relative dates, graph queries, conditionals such as IFVAR). Community description: "a supercharged template engine that contains its own simple programming language". — [RoamJS SmartBlocks repo](https://github.com/dvargas92495/roamjs-smartblocks); [SmartBlocks docs: trigger your workflow](https://github.com/RoamJs/smartblocks/blob/main/docs/010-trigger-your-workflow.md)
- SmartBlocks triggers: typing a trigger sequence (default "xx", configurable in Roam Depot settings), Roam buttons, keyboard shortcuts, automatic daily workflows, and batch execution over multiple pages. — [SmartBlocks docs: trigger your workflow](https://github.com/RoamJs/smartblocks/blob/main/docs/010-trigger-your-workflow.md)
- SmartBlocks input is limited: dropdown input is a single selector natively; a third-party extension adds multi-select. Arbitrary-JavaScript commands were deprecated on 4 July 2022. — [Universal dropdown extension](https://github.com/fbgallet/roam-extension-universal-dropdown); [SmartBlocks developer docs](https://github.com/RoamJs/smartblocks/blob/main/docs/070-developer-docs.md)
- Obsidian QuickAdd has a native sequential-prompt mode and a one-page form mode; variables already set by an earlier macro step skip their prompts; cancelling one prompt cancels the entire run (no partial/paused runs). — [QuickAdd docs: Controlling Prompts](https://quickadd.obsidian.guide/docs/ControllingPrompts/)
- QuickAdd maps Templater's `tp.system.prompt` to `{{VALUE:name}}` and `tp.system.suggester` to `{{VALUE:Option A,Option B}}`; macros chain steps such as a capture followed by a Templater command. — [QuickAdd docs: Coming from Templater](https://quickadd.obsidian.guide/docs/ComingFromTemplater); [Obsidian forum thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/timestamped-personal-logs-with-quickadd/94507)
- Templater friction: multiple `tp.system.prompt` calls do not reliably ask in order unless wrapped in an awaited execution block with variables; users write `while` loops around a suggester with a "Done" option to collect repeated answers. I.e. sequencing requires JavaScript. — [Obsidian forum: order of system prompts](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/reversing-the-order-of-system-prompts-in-templater-plugin/87736); [Obsidian forum: QuickAdd thread](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/quickadd-plugin/20032/70)
- Tana: supertags turn a node into a typed object with a template of fields; command nodes are buttons attached to nodes, supertag instances or fields, built by chaining actions, filterable by node context, and can be triggered automatically. — [Tana docs: command nodes](https://tana.inc/docs/command-nodes); [Tana docs: supertags](https://tana.inc/docs/supertags)
- Tana's prompt-oriented commands are AI commands (a "Prompt workbench", prompts referencing field values); "prompt" in Tana means an LLM prompt, not a prompt to the human (AI-dependent). — [Tana docs: AI command nodes](https://tana.inc/docs/ai-command-nodes)
- Tana templates install supertags, fields, commands and views into a workspace; changing their configuration may break the template (fragility of shared workflow packages). — [Tana docs: Tana templates](https://tana.inc/docs/tana-templates)
- Logseq: dynamic variables work in templates, queries and macros but not in custom commands or journal blocks; users report variables not being processed properly in templates and syntax confusion; a community SmartBlocks port exists with stated limits (e.g. dynamic variables only a few blocks deep). — [Logseq forum](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/make-custom-macros-that-replace-themselves-with-their-value-on-first-run/20967/12); [Logseq forum: templates injecting dynamic dates](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/templates-injecting-dynamic-dates-and-placeholders-arguments/833); [logseq-smartblocks](https://awesome.ecosyste.ms/projects/github.com%2Fsawhney17%2Flogseq-smartblocks)

### Inferences
- The spectrum found is: static template (Day One, Logseq, Capacities-style object templates) then parameterised template with input dialogs (QuickAdd, Templater) then scripted macro with branching (SmartBlocks, Tana commands). All three are "expand then forget": after insertion the result is ordinary text with no memory that it was a run, no step state, no outcome. Mindstream's run-as-object with write-back is not present in any of these.
- QuickAdd's "cancel one prompt cancels the whole run" is an anti-pattern for thinking work: a Mindstream run should be suspendable at any step (parked), since real doubt/investigation spans sessions.
- Borrowable: QuickAdd's "variables already set skip their prompts" (pre-fill step one of "Doubt" with the selected claim text); SmartBlocks' multiple trigger surfaces (inline trigger, button, shortcut) for starting a workflow from the text itself; QuickAdd's choice between sequential prompts and one-page form as a per-user display preference for short workflows.
- Tana's warning that editing an installed template may break it argues for Mindstream's variants being copies (fork-and-edit) rather than live-linked configurations.
- The dominant complaint pattern in this category is that sequencing needs code (JavaScript in Templater, command DSL in SmartBlocks). A plain ordered list of prompts authored as text is a real simplification.

### Gaps
- Notion templates/buttons, Capacities templates, Org-mode capture templates and org-babel were not verified this session. Unverified background: Notion buttons insert blocks/edit properties and database templates pre-fill pages, with no stepwise run state; Capacities has per-object-type templates only; Org capture templates support interactive `%^{prompt}` expansions that ask a series of questions at capture time and file the result at a target location (closest classic precedent for "prompted capture with write-back to a location"), and org-babel offers executable literate documents rather than guided human prompts.
- No evidence found of any tools-for-thought plugin that implements nested guided procedures or run outcomes; absence is based on limited searching, not an exhaustive plugin survey.
- Current maintenance status of SmartBlocks, QuickAdd, Templater in late 2026 not individually confirmed (QuickAdd has an open GitHub issue numbered above 1500, suggesting ongoing activity: [QuickAdd issue](https://github.com/chhoumann/quickadd/issues/1555)).

## 3. Decision-making and problem-solving tools (Untools, decision journals, Double Crux, forecasting/calibration, Guesstimate)

### Takeaway
Decision and problem-solving methods are overwhelmingly distributed as static worksheets (PDF/Notion/Miro), not as guided software; the software that does exist concentrates on predictions and estimates (Fatebook, Squiggle/Guesstimate), where the key mechanic is a mandatory later "resolve" step that produces a track record. That deferred-resolution loop is the most borrowable idea here.

### Cited Findings
- Untools is a catalogue of thinking tools sold as templates, not an app: a 2020 Notion bundle (six templates including Decision Matrix and Second-Order Thinking, launched at $9), a later "Untools for Notion: 7 templates" at $25 whose Gumroad page says it is "not currently for sale", and an "Untools Vault" membership listed at $99 with guides on choosing the right tool and templates in PDF, Miro and Notion. Each template comes with a guide including a worked example. — [Untools newsletter: launching Untools for Notion](https://buttondown.com/untools/archive/launching-untools-for-notion); [Gumroad: Untools for Notion](https://untools.gumroad.com/l/untools-notion); [Untools templates](https://www.untools.co/templates/); [Untools decision-making tools](https://www.untools.co/decision-making)
- Untools frames second-order thinking as considering "the consequences of your consequences" and pairs it with the 10-10-10 rule across time horizons. — [Untools newsletter](https://buttondown.com/untools/archive/second-order-thinking-and-the-10-10-10-rule)
- Decision journal (Farnam Street lineage): prompts reported by a secondary source are mental or physical state; situation or context; problem statement or frame; variables that govern the situation; complications or complexities; range of outcomes; expectations and probabilities; outcome (filled in later). Original at fs.blog. — [Optemization on Medium](https://medium.com/optemization/make-better-decisions-93418bcdae62); [Farnam Street: decision journal](https://fs.blog/2014/02/decision-journal/)
- A personal adaptation added an explicit "6 month review learnings" prompt; other guides recommend reviewing weeks later to compare prediction with reality. A six-month review is not confirmed as part of the official FS template. — [Personal blog](https://blackbird-sawfish-3e47.squarespace.com/blog/2022/8/27/do-you-reflect-on-decisions); [MyLifeNote decision journal guide, 2026](https://blog.mylifenote.ai/decision-journal/)
- Double Crux (CFAR): a crux is any fact such that believing differently about it would change your conclusion; a double crux is a crux for both parties. A worksheet/game version has each person write down their full list of true cruxes next to the question. — [LessWrong: Double Crux](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/exa5kmvopeRyfJgCy/double-crux-a-strategy-for-resolving-disagreement); [LessWrong wiki: double crux](https://www.lesswrong.com/w/double-crux)
- CFAR staff note that one frequently does not find a double crux and does not need to: "single cruxes are very useful". Critics argue double cruxes are rare where reasonable people differ and that benefits come from prerequisite epistemic virtues, not the technique. — [LessWrong comment from CFAR staff](https://www.lesswrong.com/lw/o6p/double_crux_a_strategy_for_resolving_disagreement/divl); [LessWrong: Contra double crux](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/nm6XuC9CzNBrthpPB/contra-double-crux)
- A solo variant is documented: "Finding cruxes: help reality punch you in the face" (using cruxes on your own beliefs to find what would change your mind). — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/wkDdQrBxoGLqPWh2P/finding-cruxes-help-reality-punch-you-in-the-face)
- Fatebook (fatebook.io, by Sage): type a prediction and hit enter; later an email reminds you to resolve it YES, NO or AMBIGUOUS; questions are private by default; resolving builds a track record with Brier score, relative Brier score and a calibration chart. Has CSV export, import from spreadsheets and PredictionBook, a browser extension, Slack integration, an unofficial Android app, and a Beeminder integration. Cloud web app. — [LessWrong: Introducing Fatebook](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track); [EA Forum: Fatebook](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/DWFRBzK3rAH3HFDZr/fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track-predictions); [Beeminder blog](https://blog.beeminder.com/fatebook); [EA Forum: unofficial Android app](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/8t363y2uXe4sEXacM/new-unofficial-fatebook-android-app)
- Users requested per-tag calibration scores; a developer began an "Enriched Fatebook" power-user view with more granular calibration. — [LessWrong: Introducing Fatebook](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)
- Scepticism about personal prediction tracking: calibration only shows up across many predictions, so a personal record stays too small to be informative for a long time; a separate post catalogues unsolved problems in grading your own predictions. — [LessWrong: What value does personal prediction tracking have?](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/Dxk4odvhTkHFZEwEZ/what-value-does-personal-prediction-tracking-have); [LessWrong: Grading your own predictions](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sfNvnRhnowKz3itCi/grading-your-own-predictions-the-parts-i-couldn-t-solve)
- Alternatives named in the same threads: Manifold, "Prior: Calibration Trainer" (iOS/Mac), "Calibrate" (open-source Android), and Anki with uncertainty. — [LessWrong: best tools for recording predictions](https://lesswrong.com/posts/HzRLuTPZYbqPau6eu/what-are-the-best-tools-for-recording-predictions); [App Store: Prior](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6761640659); [LessWrong: Introducing Calibrate](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/fuFYm3w4YAKxvhtNy/sharpening-your-map-introducing-calibrate)
- Guesstimate is reported as still maintained but with active development shifted to Squiggle and Squiggle Hub (QURI); Squiggle is a free, open-source language for probabilistic estimation, described as more powerful but less visual and harder to learn than Guesstimate, with known bugs at the time of the sources (some sources date from 2020 to 2023). — [LongtermWiki](https://www.longtermwiki.com/wiki/E1945); [LessWrong: Announcing Squiggle early access](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/mv6nuN7nhnNpj9MLr/announcing-squiggle-early-access); [EA Forum](https://forum.effectivealtruism.org/posts/TsaRbCotCaWpcrt8F)

### Inferences
- The market gap is explicit: Untools, the decision journal, Double Crux, pre-mortem, 5 Whys are all worksheets you fill in elsewhere. Their step lists are ready-made seed content for a Mindstream starter library (Decision journal, Second-order thinking, Find the crux, Pre-mortem), and Untools' practice of shipping each template with a worked example and a "which tool when" guide is a good model for library entries.
- The "Doubt" workflow's third prompt ("what would have to be true for it to fail") is essentially single-crux finding. CFAR's own retreat from requiring a double crux supports Mindstream's design that a run may legitimately end in "a sharper question" (the crux) rather than a settled call.
- Fatebook's three-way resolution (YES / NO / AMBIGUOUS) is a precedent for a small closed set of outcome types; "AMBIGUOUS" acknowledges that the question was badly posed, which is what "a sharper question" repairs.
- Deferred resolution is the borrowable mechanic: when a run ends in "a call" that contains an expectation, let the user attach a check-back date and an optional confidence; the later review compares call with reality (decision-journal loop). Keep it optional, because the cited scepticism says small personal samples give weak calibration signal and grading is hard.
- The decision journal's first field (mental/physical state) is a cheap context capture no reasoning tool includes; could be an optional opening prompt in decision-type variants.

### Gaps
- Loomio decision types, Coda decision docs, The Decision Lab frameworks, Kepner-Tregoe tools, 5 Whys/A3/8D apps, pre-mortem worksheets, Ness Labs frameworks and Metaculus personal predictions were not verified this session. Unverified background: Loomio offers proposal/poll types (consent, advice, score, ranked choice) for groups, with an outcome statement after closing, largely irrelevant to single-user use; Kepner-Tregoe (situation appraisal, problem analysis, decision analysis, potential problem analysis) and 8D are fixed multi-step procedures mostly delivered as training and enterprise forms; Metaculus allows private questions and shows personal calibration.
- The official Farnam Street template PDF was not read directly; field list is from a secondary source.
- Fatebook pricing (believed free) and late-2026 maintenance status not confirmed.
- No single-user app was found that runs 5 Whys, pre-mortem or a decision matrix as a guided sequence without AI; this absence is from limited searching.

## 4. Argument mapping and design-rationale tools: vocabulary of node types and moves, and adoption problems

### Takeaway
The vocabularies converge on a tiny set: IBIS has issue/position/argument (question, idea, pro, con), the discourse graph has question/claim/evidence/source with supports/opposes/informs, and Argdown has statements and arguments with support/attack. The research record is consistent that such formalisms fail in solo and early-stage use because of capture overhead and premature structure (Shipman and Marshall's "Formality Considered Harmful"), which argues for Mindstream keeping moves light and prose-first, with structure emerging from what the user already wrote.

### Cited Findings
- IBIS has three basic node kinds: issues, positions, and arguments. Positions answer issues; arguments support (pros) or object to (cons) positions. — [Wikipedia: Issue-based information system](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Issue_map)
- Lineage: gIBIS was developed by Jeff Conklin at MCC and commercialised in the early 1990s; Dialogue Mapping is the facilitation practice in which a facilitator records a group conversation live in IBIS on a shared display. — [Eight to Late: Issues, Ideas and Arguments](https://eight2late.com/2009/04/07/issues-ideas-and-arguments-a-communication-centric-approach-to-tackling-project-complexity); [Lucidchart: what is dialogue mapping](https://www.lucidchart.com/blog/what-is-dialogue-mapping)
- Compendium's default node types: question, answer, list view, map view, pro, con, note, decision, reference, argument. Note the explicit "decision" node. — [Wikipedia: Compendium (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium_(software))
- Shipman and Marshall, "Formality Considered Harmful": systems for design, writing and organising information tend to demand too much formality from users; the problems are cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge, premature structure and situational structure; argumentation and design-rationale systems are among their examples, drawn from the authors' own systems. — [Shipman and Marshall](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html)
- Buckingham Shum and colleagues, "Hypermedia support for argumentation-based rationale: 15 years on from gIBIS and QOC": reviews the rationale-capture problems, describes capture as the spectre haunting all design-rationale efforts, and argues it can be (and sometimes has been) overcome, citing case studies of Compendium, primarily where a skilled facilitator does the mapping. — [AcaWiki summary](https://acawiki.org/Hypermedia_support_for_argumentation-based_rationale:_15_Years_on_from_gIBIS_and_QOC); [KMi technical report KMI-05-18](https://research-archive.stem.open.ac.uk/stemra-data/uploads/files/KMI-05-18.pdf)
- QOC (Questions, Options, Criteria; MacLean et al. 1991) was claimed to improve reasoning because its structure relates logically to the design problem; an empirical study (Jorgensen and Aboulafia 1995) found students could use it and found it useful for organisation and documentation but less useful in the rough-out stage of design. — [Wikipedia: Design rationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Design_rationale); [KMi tech report 95-13](https://kmi.open.ac.uk/publications/techreport/kmi-95-13)
- Discourse Graphs (Joel Chan and collaborators): the Obsidian plugin ships node types Question, Claim, Evidence and Source and default relations supports, opposes, informs and derivedFrom; a sidebar "discourse context" panel shows, for example, which claims inform a question and which evidence supports or opposes a claim. Requires the Datacore plugin. — [Obsidian community plugin page](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/discourse-graphs); [GitHub: DiscourseGraphs/discourse-graph](https://github.com/DiscourseGraphs/discourse-graph)
- In the Roam extension, relations are recognised from ordinary writing patterns: referencing an evidence page in a block under a question makes the system formally record that the evidence informs the question (structure inferred from prose/outline rather than drawn). — [Roam discourse graph extension guide](https://oasis-lab.gitbook.io/roamresearch-discourse-graph-extension/guides/creating-discourse-relationships)
- Discourse Graphs is actively developed in 2026: monthly newsletters (August 2026: dedicated search interface for discourse nodes; October 2026: Obsidian plugin works on Obsidian mobile), plus MCP servers and a stated aim of a tool-agnostic protocol. — [DG newsletter August 2026](https://buttondown.com/DiscourseGraphs/archive/dg-news-august-2026/); [DG newsletter October 2026](https://buttondown.com/DiscourseGraphs/archive/dg-news-october-2026/)
- Argdown: plain-text syntax in which a paragraph is a "statement" (the basic element), arguments are written in angle brackets, statements with the same title form an equivalence class, and arguments can be logically reconstructed with premise-conclusion structure; support/attack relations are written inline. Open source (v1.8.x release noted on GitHub). — [Argdown syntax](https://argdown.org/syntax/); [Argdown guide: elements of an argument map](https://argdown.org/guide/elements-of-an-argument-map.html); [GitHub: argdown](https://github.com/argdown/argdown)
- Argument mapping and skill gains (van Gelder's Melbourne group, Rationale software by Austhink): studies report gains of about 0.8 SD on the California Critical Thinking Skills Test in 12 weeks of mapping-based practice, described as comparable to several years of undergraduate study; caveats include possible maturation effects and that gains appear without software too. — [PhilArchive: Twardy, Argument maps improve critical thinking](https://philarchive.org/archive/TWAAMIv1); [PhilArchive: Davies](https://philarchive.org/archive/DAVCAM-3); [Psychology Today](https://psychologytoday.com/intl/blog/thoughts-thinking/201811/improving-critical-thinking-through-argument-mapping); [No Computer Program Required](https://www.academia.edu/57006935/No_Computer_Program_Required)

### Inferences
- Mapping Mindstream's moves onto the established vocabularies: doubt = con/opposes/attack; support = pro/supports; branch = alternative position/option (IBIS position, QOC option); experiment and "investigate" = evidence/informs; clarify and reframe = restating the issue (Conklin-style "question the question"); resolve = Compendium's decision node; implications = QOC-style consequence, not present in IBIS; connect = generic link. "Clarify", "reframe" and "implications" have no standard node type in IBIS, discourse graphs or Argdown: Mindstream's move set is richer on the reformulation side and the established schemes are richer on evidence/source.
- All the formal schemes are typed-node graphs. Mindstream's workflow-as-ordered-prompts is a procedural layer the schemes lack: IBIS says what kinds of things exist, not what to do next. Conversely, the discourse graph's trick of deriving relations from ordinary indented writing is the design to copy if Mindstream ever wants a derived map: let the move that was taken tag the relation, never ask the user to draw it.
- The adoption literature gives three concrete guardrails: (1) never require typing before writing (premature structure); (2) keep the cost of a move at or below the cost of just typing the next sentence (cognitive overhead); (3) QOC's finding that structure helps organisation and documentation but not rough-out suggests workflows should be opt-in on a piece of existing text, not the default mode of writing. Mindstream's "moves toward your own text" already fits this.
- Dialogue Mapping succeeded mainly with a dedicated facilitator doing the capture. In single-user use the thinker is also the mapper, which is the worst case for overhead; this is a risk for nested workflows in particular (deep nesting is structure the user must hold in mind).
- The argument-mapping learning results support the deliberate-practice framing: the benefit came from repeated structured practice with feedback, not from the artefact.

### Gaps
- Kialo, Rationale/ReasoningLab (current product state), MindMup argument visualisation, DebateGraph, DRL and Toulmin-model tools were not verified this session. Unverified background: Kialo structures a debate as a thesis with nested pro/con claims and impact voting and is cloud and group/education oriented; Rationale has been succeeded by web-based tools from the same lineage; MindMup has a free argument-visualisation mode developed with academic philosophers; Toulmin's model uses claim, grounds, warrant, backing, qualifier, rebuttal (the "qualifier" and "rebuttal" slots are close analogues of Mindstream's "stated caveat"); DRL (Lee and Lai) adds goals and alternatives evaluated against goals.
- Whether Compendium is discontinued was not confirmed. Unverified background: the original Open University project ended years ago with a community fork (CompendiumNG) that also appears dormant.
- Conklin's own texts (Dialogue Mapping book, "Wicked Problems and Social Complexity") and Kirsh and Maglio on epistemic actions were not consulted directly. Unverified background: Kirsh and Maglio (1994) distinguish epistemic actions (performed to make thinking easier, e.g. rotating a Tetris piece to see it) from pragmatic actions; this supports treating Mindstream "moves" as cheap epistemic actions on text.
- No user-sentiment data gathered from r/Zettelkasten or r/RoamResearch on discourse-graph overhead in solo use.

## 5. Open-question tracking and "sharper question" outcomes

### Takeaway
Treating an unresolved question as a first-class artefact has two good precedents: the discourse graph, where Question is a primary node type that claims and evidence "inform", and the Rust RFC process, where "Unresolved questions" is a required template section that is carried forward into a tracking issue and must be answered at stabilization. No tool found enforces a hard gate with Mindstream's four dispositions (address, accept as stated caveat, lift, park) for a single user; that mechanic appears to be novel.

### Cited Findings
- The Rust RFC template has an "Unresolved questions" section asking which parts of the design the author expects to resolve through the RFC process (and which before stabilization). — [Rust RFC template](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md)
- A later meta-RFC added a separate, optional "Future possibilities" section after "Unresolved questions", noting that items there do not justify accepting the RFC: a formal separation between "open and must be settled" and "out of scope for now". — [RFC 2561: future possibilities](https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/2561-future-possibilities.html)
- The Rust tracking-issue template carries open questions forward: maintainers list all "unresolved questions" from the RFC "to ensure they are not forgotten". — [Rust tracking issue template](https://rust.googlesource.com/rust/+/HEAD/.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/tracking_issue.md)
- The stabilization report template requires stating what questions were left unresolved by the RFC, how they have been answered, with links to the relevant decisions: an actual gate at the final level, not at acceptance. — [rustc dev guide: stabilization report template](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/stabilization-report-template.html)
- Other projects copy the pattern with stronger wording, e.g. "which parts of the design must be resolved through the RFC process before this gets merged". — [Patina RFC template](https://opendevicepartnership.github.io/patina/rfc/template.html)
- Discourse graph: Question is a default node type alongside Claim, Evidence and Source; the context panel answers "what claims inform this question", so an open question accumulates its partial answers. — [Obsidian community plugin page](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/discourse-graphs)
- IBIS/Compendium: the issue (question) is the root element and maps grow by posing further questions on positions and arguments; Compendium adds a distinct "decision" node type to mark what was settled. — [Wikipedia: Issue-based information system](https://en.wikipedia.com/wiki/Issue_map); [Wikipedia: Compendium (software)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium_(software))
- Fatebook's AMBIGUOUS resolution and CBT apps' reminders about open records are lightweight acknowledgements of unfinished or ill-posed items. — [LessWrong: Introducing Fatebook](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track); [App Store: Balm](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789923194)
- CFAR's observation that a crux search often ends without a double crux but with a useful single crux is a documented case of "a sharper question" being the valued output of a structured method. — [LessWrong comment from CFAR staff](https://www.lesswrong.com/lw/o6p/double_crux_a_strategy_for_resolving_disagreement/divl)

### Inferences
- The Rust process maps almost one-to-one onto Mindstream's gate dispositions: answered at stabilization = addressed; listed in the tracking issue = lifted to a higher level; moved to "Future possibilities" = parked; documented limitation in the accepted text = accepted as a stated caveat. This is strong evidence the four-way disposition is a natural partition, and it is the best external justification to cite for the gate.
- Key design detail to borrow from Rust: the gate bites at the last level (stabilization), while intermediate levels only require that questions be listed and carried. Mindstream could make "lift" the low-friction default at inner levels and reserve strict resolution for the outermost conclusion, to limit the overhead that the design-rationale literature warns about.
- Borrow from the discourse graph: an open question should show what already "informs" it (the steps and notes written under it), so that a run ending in "a sharper question" visibly carries its partial progress when it is written back at the originating text.
- Borrow from RFC 2561: keep "parked" visibly distinct from "caveat". A caveat qualifies the conclusion and travels with it; a parked item does not.
- In every precedent found the gate is social (reviewers enforce it). A self-enforced software gate for one person is untested: risk of users rubber-stamping "park" to get past it. Worth making the disposition cost one keystroke but always leaving a visible trace on the conclusion (for example "concluded with 2 caveats, 1 parked").

### Gaps
- ADR formats (Nygard, MADR) status fields and whether they include open-question sections were not verified. Unverified background: ADRs carry a status (proposed, accepted, deprecated, superseded) and a "consequences" section; open questions are not a standard field.
- Research/lab notebook apps (electronic lab notebooks, Jupyter-style notebooks) and issue trackers were not researched for blocking semantics. Unverified background: issue trackers offer "blocked by" dependencies and required checks before merge, the nearest software analogue of a hard gate, but in a team setting.
- No source found for assumption-tracking attached to a conclusion in personal tools (beyond Toulmin's qualifier/rebuttal, unverified). No comparable single-user feature found for "cannot conclude a level while questions started there are undecided".

## 6. Deliberate-practice angle: practising a thinking method repeatedly and comparing runs over time

### Takeaway
Run-over-run comparison exists only for predictions (Fatebook's Brier score and calibration chart, decision-journal reviews) and for memory (spaced repetition); no tool found lets a person compare repeated runs of the same thinking method. Matuschak's work points toward spaced prompts for application and reflection rather than recall, but Orbit remains a research vehicle.

### Cited Findings
- Fatebook builds a track record as forecasts are resolved: Brier score, relative Brier score and a calibration chart; users asked for per-tag calibration to compare domains. — [LessWrong: Introducing Fatebook](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)
- Counterpoint: calibration needs many resolved predictions, so a personal record stays uninformative for a long time; grading one's own predictions has unsolved problems. — [LessWrong: What value does personal prediction tracking have?](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/Dxk4odvhTkHFZEwEZ/what-value-does-personal-prediction-tracking-have); [LessWrong: Grading your own predictions](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/sfNvnRhnowKz3itCi/grading-your-own-predictions-the-parts-i-couldn-t-solve)
- Dedicated calibration trainers exist: "Prior: Calibration Trainer" (iOS/Mac) and the open-source Android app "Calibrate". — [App Store: Prior](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6761640659); [LessWrong: Introducing Calibrate](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/fuFYm3w4YAKxvhtNy/sharpening-your-map-introducing-calibrate)
- Decision-journal practice is explicitly a compare-later loop: record expected outcomes and probabilities now, fill in the actual outcome later and review. — [Farnam Street: decision journal](https://fs.blog/2014/02/decision-journal/); [Alliance for Decision Education: keeping a decision journal](https://alliancefordecisioneducation.org/resources/keeping-a-decision-journal/)
- Orbit (Andy Matuschak) is "an experimental spaced repetition platform", "primarily a vehicle for research", supporting the mnemonic medium (spaced repetition embedded in prose, first used in Quantum Country); offered as a free service. — [GitHub: andymatuschak/orbit](https://github.com/andymatuschak/orbit); [Matuschak notes: Mnemonic medium](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/Mnemonic_medium)
- Matuschak reports experimenting, in the last chapter of Quantum Country, with spaced-repetition prompts that help readers apply what they learned, not just remember it; and criticises the original medium for putting the author in the driver's seat (assumes linear, complete reading), proposing redesign around reader control. — [Matuschak, 2020 Patreon update](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/z88DbhHNrGchNZSndjgw3sF); [Matuschak, 2021 letter on reader control](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/zNXS7UkMmGYTFRyJCeLnEoH)
- Matuschak's guide to writing prompts is the standard reference on prompt craft for spaced repetition. — [How to write good prompts](https://andymatuschak.org/prompts/)
- Argument-mapping studies attribute critical-thinking gains (about 0.8 SD in 12 weeks) to sustained, structured practice. — [PhilArchive: Twardy](https://philarchive.org/archive/TWAAMIv1)
- CBT thought records rate emotion and belief before and after (0 to 100), giving a per-run delta that can be reviewed over time. — [App Store: Balm](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789923194); [Psychology Tools: thought records](https://www.psychologytools.com/self-help/thought-records)

### Inferences
- Because every Mindstream run ends in a typed outcome and belongs to a named workflow variant, a run history view comes almost for free: per workflow, count of runs, ratio of "call" to "sharper question", number of caveats/parked items per conclusion, and (if the user set a check-back) whether calls held up. No comparable view was found anywhere; this would be distinctive.
- Borrow the timeful/spaced idea without AI or recall cards: resurface (a) calls with a check-back date, (b) parked items, and (c) "sharper questions" after an interval, as prompts to re-run or resolve. This uses spacing for reflection on the user's own statements, which is the direction Matuschak gestures at.
- Matuschak's reader-control critique applies to workflows: a fixed author-ordered sequence feels coercive; allow skipping and reordering steps in a run, and let a variant be saved from how the user actually ran it.
- Be cautious about quantified self-scoring: the prediction-tracking community's own scepticism (small samples, grading ambiguity) suggests descriptive history rather than scores.

### Gaps
- Readwise (daily review, spaced resurfacing of highlights) and Mochi (Markdown flashcards with templates) were not verified this session; both are recall/resurfacing tools and, as unverified background, neither supports practising a reasoning procedure.
- Orbit's status after 2021 was not confirmed; newest material found dates from 2021. Treat as possibly dormant.
- No evidence found (either way) on whether repeated solo use of a fixed questioning routine improves judgment outside classroom argument-mapping studies.
