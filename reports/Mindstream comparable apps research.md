# Mindstream's mechanics have precedents, not peers

No single app in the research notes occupies Mindstream's position, and most of its individual mechanics have only partial precedents: the nearest neighbours are Workflowy and Org-mode for drilling, Ulysses and Scrivener for a separate revision phase, Llama Life, Amazing Marvin, Sunsama and Super Productivity for one-task-at-a-time execution, Stoic, Day One and CBT thought-record apps for ordered prompts, and VS Code, Android, FDC3 and JupyterLab for the planned shell. Mindstream aligns with the field on its outer shapes (zoom with a breadcrumb, a timer on a single task, prompt templates, a registry-driven "send to" menu) and departs from it on what happens inside them: a drilled span opens as a blank page, the converged result replaces the span, a gate forces a decision on every open item, decomposition pushes a stack instead of growing a list, and a takeaway lands on the parent. For those five mechanics the researchers found no shipped equivalent, though that is a statement about a limited search, not proof of absence. The most valuable things to borrow are small and concrete: a one-line "ready-to-resume" note when parking, a gate message that names the undecided items, an open-items panel in Reflect, a persisted pending record for ASK, and Android's rules for an uncluttered share menu. The comparables also expose real risks: cheap tagging that becomes a guilt backlog, a self-enforced gate that users switch off or rubber-stamp, formal structure imposed before the thinking is done, and a self-registering "Send to…" list that fills with clutter. Readers should weigh all of this against the evidence base: the session's network blocked many primary sites, so much of what follows rests on search-result summaries, several named products could not be verified, and no price was confirmed on an official page.

## The evidence is search-summary deep, so absences are provisional

All four research strands report the same constraint. Direct fetches failed for most vendor and primary sites (among them intend.do, llamalife.co, blitzit.app, taskwarrior.org, the Amazing Marvin help centre, notes.andymatuschak.org, inkandswitch.com, allume.com, code.visualstudio.com, jupyterlab.readthedocs.io and the x-callback-url site). Pages read in full were few: Super Productivity's GitHub README, Android's developer documentation, and the FDC3 2.2 specification via its GitHub source. Everything else is "as surfaced by a search summary of the cited page", which means wording is paraphrased and details such as exact layouts, nesting limits and menu labels are unconfirmed.

Three consequences shape how this report should be read. First, **"no comparable feature found" means not found in a limited search of roughly a dozen products per area**, never "does not exist"; each such claim below carries that meaning even where the sentence is short. Second, **no pricing figure was confirmed on an official pricing page**, and the aggregator figures the researchers collected disagree with each other, so this report gives no prices and does not compare on cost. Third, a long list of products named in the brief or by the researchers could not be verified at all and are deliberately left out of the findings: zoom and side-panel behaviour in Roam, Logseq, Tana, Dynalist, Bike, Notion, Capacities and Scrintal; 750 Words; Things 3, Todoist, Akiflow, Motion, Structured, Sukha and Flow Club as execution tools; Checkvist; Reflectly, Journey, Reflection.app, Daylio and the Five Minute Journal; Kialo, Rationale, MindMup and the current state of Compendium; Notion, Anytype, Tana, TiddlyWiki and Coda as host platforms; Gladys, clipboard managers and Readwise Reader as shelves or inboxes. The researchers hold background beliefs about many of these, but none was sourced in the session, so they are not presented here as fact. One named feature, Amazing Marvin "procedures", could not be matched to any real feature at all.

The researchers' statements about what Mindstream itself does (for example its set of "moves" and a "Doubt" workflow) come from the project brief, not from this research, and are used here only where the brief given for this report confirms them.

## ThinkPad borrows the outliner's zoom but changes what zooming means

**Drilling into a node with a breadcrumb is a mature, standard pattern.** Workflowy builds a clickable breadcrumb trail on zoom and collapses long paths into an ellipsis ([Workflowy help](https://workflowy.com/help/sidebar-navigation)), and its panes give "another view of an existing item, not a duplicate", each with its own breadcrumbs and history ([Workflowy panes](https://workflowy.com/help/panes)), which is the closest sourced analogue to ThinkPad's parent peek. Org-mode offers both narrowing to a subtree and an indirect buffer whose edits change the original "without affecting visibility in that buffer" ([Org manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/manual/html_node/org/Global-and-local-cycling.html)). Heptabase nests sub-whiteboards and lists a "sub-whiteboard preview mode" on its roadmap for November 2026 ([Heptabase roadmap](https://wiki.heptabase.com/roadmap)), and Muse nests boards inside boards ([Setapp listing](https://setapp.com/apps/628)). The difference is the contract: every sourced tool zooms into the node's existing content, while ThinkPad opens an empty sub-pad with the source span held as the prompt. No tool with that behaviour was found. A Zettelkasten forum practice supports the choice, since at least one practitioner treats fleeting notes only as prompts to write fresh notes from scratch, never as drafts to edit ([Zettelkasten Forum](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/7509)).

The classic worry about nested navigation is disorientation. Conklin's 1987 survey named two hypertext problems, losing one's location and the "cognitive overhead" of maintaining several trails at once ([Conklin 1987](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~andre/informatics223s2017/conklin.pdf)). ThinkPad's drill is a strict tree, which a breadcrumb describes completely, so the researchers judge it the benign case; a goal reminder addresses the overhead problem that breadcrumbs alone do not. That reasoning is an inference, and the researchers found no evidence either way about users getting lost in deep outliner zoom specifically.

**Resolve is where ThinkPad departs furthest from the field.** Every sourced mechanism for relating child content to a parent either keeps the two identical or makes a one-off copy. Workflowy mirrors "keep them perfectly in sync" ([Workflowy mirrors](https://workflowy.com/learn/mirrors)); Notion synced blocks reflect a change everywhere ([Notion Academy](https://notion.com/help/notion-academy/lesson/synced-blocks)); Logseq distinguishes a reference as "a window" from an embed as "a portal" ([Logseq forum](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/the-difference-between-logseqs-block-embeds-and-block-references/8459)); Roam can flatten a block reference into plain text, an embed, an alias, or text plus alias, as described in a forum thread that may predate current menus ([Outliner Software forum](https://www.outlinersoftware.com/topics/viewt/9127)). Roam's "replace with text" is the nearest operation to resolve, but it copies the whole block as it stands. ThinkPad's version is reductive: the child must converge to one idea, that idea replaces a span, and the working-out stays out of the text. The researchers found nothing equivalent, and nothing in any note tool resembling the condition that text must shrink to a single paragraph before it can fold back.

**Stream-then-reflect exists elsewhere only as two separate products or an opt-in mode.** The Most Dangerous Writing App enforces forward motion by deleting everything after five idle seconds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Most_Dangerous_Writing_App)). Ulysses' revision mode, in sources from 2020 and 2021, dims the editor and gathers the writer's own annotations and comments into one place ([MacRumors forum](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/ulysses-21-brings-advanced-grammar-and-style-check-to-iphone-and-ipad.2258041)). Scrivener offers manual snapshots with a compare view at three levels of granularity ([Well-Storied](https://www.well-storied.com/blog/how-to-review-old-drafts-with-scrivener-snapshots)), and its users ask for whole-document version history and a "rewrite mode" that keeps the old scene visible ([Literature & Latte forum](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/version-history/124722)). No sourced tool freezes a draft and then restricts change to structured operations; ThinkPad's stamp is that one-way door, and it enforces the phase split structurally where the "danger" apps use a penalty. The one web app built around draft-by-draft versioning, Draft, closed at the end of 2022 ([Beeminder help](https://help.beeminder.com/article/121-draft)).

**Tagging your own paragraphs with a fixed vocabulary of intents was not found**; the nearest is, again, Ulysses collecting annotations into a revision hub. Annotation-then-process is well established for other people's text, and its known failure is instructive. Tiago Forte's own guidance says it is "absolutely NOT the goal to stuff every single note through all the layers" ([Forte Labs](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization/)), and Nick Milo's critique names the outcome as "over-collecting, over-summarization and under thinking" ([Milo on Medium](https://medium.com/@nickmilo22/why-progressive-summarization-must-die-c2635d1f79f1)). ThinkPad has two structural defences the surveyed tools lack: the corpus is one bounded draft, and the gate forces each tag to a decision.

**The open-item gate has one true software precedent and one strong procedural one.** Org-mode, with `org-enforce-todo-dependencies` enabled, blocks an entry from becoming DONE while it has undone TODO children, with a `NOBLOCKING` property as a per-node exemption ([Org manual](https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/manual/html_node/org/TODO-dependencies.html)). Issue trackers roll up without blocking: Linear can auto-close a parent when all sub-issues are done ([Linear changelog](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-09-06-auto-close-parent-and-sub-issues)), and Jira reaches the same result through automation rules ([Atlassian KB](https://support.atlassian.com/automation/kb/close-the-parent-issue-when-all-its-sub-tasks-are-closed/)). No writing or thinking tool with any completion gate was found. The procedural precedent is the Rust RFC process, where "Unresolved questions" is a template section ([Rust RFC template](https://github.com/rust-lang/rfcs/blob/master/0000-template.md)), open questions are carried into a tracking issue "to ensure they are not forgotten" ([tracking issue template](https://rust.googlesource.com/rust/+/HEAD/.github/ISSUE_TEMPLATE/tracking_issue.md)), a stabilization report must say how each was answered ([rustc dev guide](https://rustc-dev-guide.rust-lang.org/stabilization-report-template.html)), and a separate "Future possibilities" section holds items that are out of scope for now ([RFC 2561](https://rust-lang.github.io/rfcs/2561-future-possibilities.html)). The researchers map these onto Mindstream's four dispositions almost one to one: answered is address, carried to the tracking issue is lift, future possibilities is park, and a documented limitation is a stated caveat. That mapping is their inference, but it is the best external argument that the four-way split is a natural partition.

## The Focus runner inverts the list-first norm of task apps

**The dominant execution pattern is "one task, a countdown, and a peek at what is next", fed from a pre-planned list.** Llama Life describes itself as helping you "focus on ONE task at a time" with a countdown on every task ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)). Amazing Marvin's Super Focus Mode shows the day's tasks one after another and sits at the end of a narrowing funnel from master list to focus ([Marvin help](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/2237484-super-focus-mode-sfm)). Sunsama's focus mode, per third-party reviews, hides other tasks and allows sub-tasks and notes inside it ([The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/some-first-impressions-of-sunsama-the-focus-course-in-app-form/)). Tiimo and Routinery walk through steps with a timer each ([Tiimo](https://www.tiimoapp.com/product/visual-planner); [Routinery blog](https://www.routinery.app/blog/best-routine-apps-with-timer-and-progress)). Super Productivity, the one verified local-first comparable in this group, offers "Pomodoro, Flowtime and countdown sessions" and keeps data "on your device unless you choose to sync it" ([GitHub README](https://github.com/super-productivity/super-productivity)). Mindstream aligns with all of these on single-tasking with a timer. It differs in being focus-first: the user types what they are doing now and structure grows downward. None of the sources describes a view that shows the current task with its chain of parent goals, or one that pushes a smaller focus on top of the current one mid-execution.

**Decomposition in the moment is now mostly sold as an AI feature.** Goblin Tools' Magic ToDo generates steps at a chosen granularity and lets every item be broken down further ([Gigazine hands-on](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250326-magic-todo)); Llama Life's "Break it down" and Tiimo's checklists do the same with AI ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750); [Tiimo](https://tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/ai-planner)). Manual sub-tasks exist everywhere but as a planning gesture inside a list. OmniFocus comes closest to turning an outline into a one-at-a-time stream, because later actions in a sequential project stay blocked until earlier ones complete ([OmniFocus 4 manual](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/perspectives/)). Mindstream's human-authored, one-line stack push is counter-positioned against the AI trend, and no manual "push, then return to the parent" mechanic was found.

**No app was found that asks for a takeaway on completion and files it on the parent.** The analogues work at other grains: Session records "what have you learned" per focus session ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/session-pomodoro-focus-timer/id1521432881)), Focusmate partners report how the session went ([Focusmate help](https://support.focusmate.com/en/articles/4044432-what-happens-during-a-focusmate-session-do-s-don-ts)), Sunsama runs an evening shutdown review ([Saner.ai review](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-reviews)), and Intend's founder describes a "fractal review system" that synthesizes daily through yearly reflections per goal ([Malcolm Ocean](https://malcolmocean.com/2022/11/my-resistances-to-growing-complice/)), the clearest example of lower-level outcomes rolling into a higher-level record. The practice Mindstream most resembles is interstitial journaling, a few lines written at every task switch ([Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/interstitial-journaling)); the takeaway is that habit made structural, prompted at the switch point and stored on the parent.

**Parking has three established mechanisms, all applied to single tasks.** A tag or list removes the item from "available" views (OmniFocus On Hold tags, per its [manual](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/perspectives/) and user forum); a date hides it until it resurfaces (Taskwarrior's `wait:`, per [its docs](https://taskwarrior.org/docs/using_dates/); FacileThings follow-up dates, per [its blog](https://facilethings.com/blog/en/deadlines-and-expected-dates)); or a dependency unblocks it automatically (Linear's "Blocked by" flag that turns green when the blocker resolves, per [Linear docs](https://linear.app/docs/issue-relations); Marvin's dependency strategy, per [Marvin help](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/2628279-dependencies)). Jira's flag marks an impediment without changing the card's status or position ([Atlassian](https://confluence.atlassian.com/display/JIRASOFTWARECLOUD/Flagging+an+issue)), which matches Mindstream's choice to mark a context and preserve its stack. Parking an entire working context with a free-text reason has no direct counterpart in the sources. The research backing is stronger than the product precedent: Leroy and Glomb (2018) found that briefly writing a "ready-to-resume" plan, where you stopped and what comes next, reduced attention residue and protected performance on the interrupting task, with the limitation that the studies did not test performance on the original task after return ([UW News](https://www.washington.edu/news/2018/01/16/task-interrupted-a-plan-for-returning-helps-you-move-on/)). Mindstream's "waiting on" reason captures half of such a plan.

## Workflows sit in a gap between worksheets, journals and macros

**Ordered prompts producing one entry is a consumer pattern, and user-authored prompt sets exist.** Stoic's release notes describe custom journals built from the user's own prompts ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/stoic/id1312926037)); Day One separates user-made templates from rotating daily prompts and lets a template be bound to a reminder ([Day One guide](https://dayoneapp.com/guides/tips-and-tutorials/templates/)). The closest structural precedent is the CBT thought record, which the NHS presents as "a set of 7 prompts" ([NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/every-mind-matters/mental-wellbeing-tips/self-help-cbt-techniques/thought-record)) ending in a single balanced thought, with belief and emotion rated before and after, and with the explicit allowance that the feeling need not drop to zero ([Psychology Tools](https://www.psychologytools.com/self-help/thought-records)). One app, Balm, "gently reminds you about open records" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6789923194)). None of the journaling apps found treats a run as ending in a typed outcome, nests one prompt set inside a step of another, or writes a result back to the text that triggered it.

**Tools for thought stop at "expand, then forget".** Roam SmartBlocks, Obsidian QuickAdd and Templater, and Tana command nodes chain steps and ask for inputs, but the result is ordinary text with no memory of having been a run. QuickAdd cancels the whole run if one prompt is cancelled ([QuickAdd docs](https://quickadd.obsidian.guide/docs/ControllingPrompts/)); Templater needs JavaScript to ask questions in order ([Obsidian forum](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/reversing-the-order-of-system-prompts-in-templater-plugin/87736)); Tana warns that reconfiguring an installed template may break it ([Tana docs](https://tana.inc/docs/tana-templates)), and in Tana "prompt" means an LLM prompt ([Tana docs](https://tana.inc/docs/ai-command-nodes)). A workflow written as a plain ordered list of prompts, run as a persistent object that can be suspended, is not present in any of these. Two existing models bracket the design: timed routine runners such as Routinery and Tiimo present one step at a time but capture nothing, while team checklist products such as Process Street, Manifestly and Tallyfy capture data per step but are business software known here only through vendor comparison pages ([Process Street](https://www.process.st/process-street-vs-tallyfy/)). A personal runner whose steps produce text and can hand off to a writing tool was not found.

**Thinking methods themselves ship as worksheets, not software.** Untools sells templates for Notion, Miro and PDF, each with a worked example ([Untools](https://www.untools.co/templates/)); the decision journal is a list of fields with an outcome filled in later ([Farnam Street](https://fs.blog/2014/02/decision-journal/)); Double Crux is a worksheet practice in which CFAR staff concede one often ends without a double crux and that "single cruxes are very useful" ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/lw/o6p/double_crux_a_strategy_for_resolving_disagreement/divl)). That concession is the best documented case of "a sharper question" being a legitimate ending. Where guided software exists it concentrates on predictions: Fatebook reminds the user later to resolve a forecast YES, NO or AMBIGUOUS and builds a calibration record ([LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/yS3d46m23wRKDQobt/introducing-fatebook-the-fastest-way-to-make-and-track)), a small closed set of outcome types with a deferred check.

**Argument-mapping research is a warning about formality.** IBIS, Compendium, discourse graphs and Argdown converge on a tiny vocabulary of questions, positions, and supporting or opposing arguments, and Compendium adds an explicit decision node ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compendium_(software))). Shipman and Marshall's "Formality Considered Harmful" attributes the failure of such systems to cognitive overhead, tacit knowledge, premature structure and situational structure ([Shipman and Marshall](https://people.engr.tamu.edu/shipman/formality-paper/harmful.html)), and a study of QOC found it useful for organisation and documentation but less so in the rough-out stage ([KMi report](https://kmi.open.ac.uk/publications/techreport/kmi-95-13)). The Roam discourse-graph extension shows the alternative: it infers relations from ordinary indented writing instead of asking the user to draw them ([extension guide](https://oasis-lab.gitbook.io/roamresearch-discourse-graph-extension/guides/creating-discourse-relationships)). Mindstream's prose-first stream, with structure applied afterwards to text that already exists, sits on the right side of this literature; nested workflows are the part most exposed to it.

## The planned shell follows patterns that platforms kept after reversing others

The workspace research is the area where Mindstream is least novel and best supported. **The proven pattern is "declare statically, activate lazily, invoke by command id".** VS Code extensions publish commands and menu placements in a manifest and use `when` clauses over host-owned context keys to control availability ([VS Code docs](https://code.visualstudio.com/api/references/when-clause-contexts)); FDC3 apps register the intents they handle in an app-directory record ([FDC3 2.2 spec](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/finos/FDC3/main/website/versioned_docs/version-2.2/api/spec.md)); Raycast makes the first action in every item's panel the default on Return ([Raycast API](https://developers.raycast.com/api-reference/user-interface/action-panel)). Tools rarely call each other directly. Mindstream's capability registry and dumb broker are this pattern.

**Android draws the same line Mindstream draws between SEND and ASK.** Its Sharesheet is "primarily designed for sending content outside your app", while the intent resolver "is best suited for passing data to the next stage of a well-defined task" and simply runs when only one app matches ([Android docs](https://developer.android.com/training/sharing/send)). The same page supplies a rule set for a share menu that stays usable: filter by the most specific content type, preview what is being sent, let the system rank by usage, cap custom targets, exclude the sender's own targets, and land directly where the action happens with no disambiguation step ([Android Direct Share](https://developer.android.com/training/sharing/direct-share-targets)). macOS Services is a precedent for request-and-return on selected text, where the processed result replaces the selection ([Nil Coalescing](https://nilcoalescing.com/blog/macOSSystemWideServices)), close to an ASK that returns to its origin. The Plan 9 plumber is the opposite philosophy, routing by rules with no chooser at all ([plumb(6)](https://src.rcs.uwaterloo.ca/xref/plan9/sys/man/6/plumb)).

**Every mature request-and-return system treats delivery and result as separately failing stages and keeps cancel distinct from error.** FDC3 defines separate resolution and result errors, including `UserCancelled`, `TargetInstanceUnavailable` and `NoResultReturned` ([FDC3 errors](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/finos/FDC3/main/website/versioned_docs/version-2.2/api/ref/Errors.md)); x-callback-url passes success, error and cancel URLs plus `x-source`, the caller's friendly name ([Apple Shortcuts guide](https://support.apple.com/guide/shortcuts/apdcd7f20a6f/)); Android decouples the result callback from the launch site because the calling process is "almost certain" to be destroyed in memory-heavy cases, and requires extra state to be saved separately ([Android docs](https://developer.android.com/training/basics/intents/result)). No standard sets a deadline for a human-paced answer: FDC3's 15-second default covers only launch and listener registration.

**Layout restoration works best when the shell saves how to recreate a pane, not the pane.** JupyterLab restores plugins by re-executing a registered command with saved JSON arguments, names workspaces in the URL, and offers `?reset` for recovery ([JupyterLab docs](https://jupyterlab.readthedocs.io/en/latest/user/urls.html)). For shelves the sourced evidence is thin: Dropover and Yoink are transit shelves summoned by the act of dragging ([Dropover](https://apps.apple.com/app/dropover-easier-drag-drop/id1355679052?mt=12); [Yoink](https://apps.apple.com/app/id457622435)), and the only codified expiry rule found comes from an adjacent domain, Android's instruction not to publish share targets with no activity in 30 days. On the larger design choice, Ink & Switch argues for tools over shared user-owned data while conceding that maintaining interoperability is an open question ([Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/essay/malleable-software/), via search summary), and the "smart endpoints and dumb pipes" position favours a router-only middle ([Nordic APIs](https://nordicapis.com/what-does-smart-endpoints-and-dumb-pipes-mean/)). Passing two simple shapes through a dumb broker trades live references for independence, a reasonable trade for one developer. No first-person retrospective from a solo developer who built a plugin host was found, so the solo-developer question is answered only by inference from platform histories.

## Mechanic by mechanic: what exists, where, and what was not found

Read across the rows, the pattern is consistent. Where Mindstream's mechanic is a container or a conduit, a comparable exists. Where it is a rule about how thinking must proceed (blank page on drill, shrink before fold-back, decide every open item, push instead of plan, record what you learned on the way up), the search found none. That is also where Mindstream's positioning is distinctive at the product level: the researchers found no product combining local-first storage, single-user scope, no AI generation and a focus-first stack. Super Productivity shares the values but not the mechanics; Llama Life and Goblin Tools share the purpose but rely on cloud accounts and AI. Several current products are moving the other way, with Stoic adapting prompts by AI, Rosebud built as an AI dialogue ([Macaron review](https://macaron.im/he/blog/rosebud-ai-journaling-app-review-2026)) and Tana's prompt commands aimed at models. The one notable ally is iA Writer, whose Authorship feature marks and dims text the writer did not write ([iA Writer support](https://ia.net/writer/support/editor/authorship)).

## The comparables point to seven risks

**Cheap tagging can become a backlog.** The progressive-summarization critique is that marking feels like progress and produces obligations nobody processes. If tagging in Reflect is too easy, users meet a wall of decisions at the gate. Making "park" and "accept as caveat" first-class and guilt-free, and showing the open count while tagging, are the mitigations the sources suggest.

**A self-enforced gate is untested.** Every gate precedent found is either opt-in and sometimes switched off (Org-mode) or enforced by reviewers (Rust). For one person the failure modes are disabling it or rubber-stamping "park". Rust's detail that the strict check bites only at the final level, with inner levels merely carrying questions forward, suggests making "lift" the low-friction default below the outermost conclusion.

**Structure can arrive too early.** The design-rationale literature found formal schemes failing through capture overhead and premature structure, and succeeding mainly where a dedicated facilitator did the mapping. In single-user use the thinker is also the mapper. Workflows should stay opt-in on existing text, a move should cost no more than typing the next sentence, and deep nesting of workflows is the feature most likely to breach that limit.

**Prompt sets wear out and fixed sequences coerce.** Stoic reviewers complain of repeated prompts, and Matuschak criticised his own medium for putting the author in the driver's seat. A user-owned library with variants answers the first; skippable, reorderable steps answer the second. Quantified self-scoring is a related trap: the forecasting community's own verdict is that a personal record stays too small to be informative for a long time ([LessWrong](https://greaterwrong.com/posts/Dxk4odvhTkHFZEwEZ/what-value-does-personal-prediction-tracking-have)), which argues for descriptive run history over scores.

**Send menus, callbacks and saved layouts each have a documented failure.** The iOS share sheet and macOS Services menu both drew clutter complaints because apps register themselves ([Six Colors](https://sixcolors.com/?p=19105); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Services_menu)). Drafts users report a callback that "hangs if app does not return" ([Drafts forum](https://forums.getdrafts.com/t/callbackurl-hangs-if-app-does-not-return/2633)). JupyterLab users recover from stuck workspaces only by resetting them ([NCAR archive](https://ncar.github.io/zulip-archive/stream/16-jupyterlab-hub/topic/resume.20jupyterhub.20with.20previously.20opened.20tabs.html)). A shelf with no expiry is the same problem in a fourth form.

**The broker can grow, and copies drift.** The critique that produced "dumb pipes" was aimed at middleware that accumulated routing and transformation logic. Message passing also means copies instead of live references; carrying provenance in the envelope softens that without a shared model. Obsidian's API changelog, with removed functions and long-standing developer confusion ([changelog](https://unpkg.com/obsidian@1.13.1/CHANGELOG.md)), shows that any exposed surface becomes a compatibility burden, which argues for a tiny, versioned registry contract.

**The category is unstable, and the novelty claims are fragile.** Draft closed in 2022, Woebot retired its app in June 2025 ([STAT News](https://www.statnews.com/2025/07/02/woebot-mental-health-chatbot-shuts-down-founder-says-ai-moving-faster-than-regulators/)), Centered is reported bankrupt on a marketplace listing ([AppSumo](https://appsumo.com/products/centered/reviews/)), Arc is reported to sunset in December 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/22/arc-browser-sunsets-december-22-as-the-browser-company-doubles-down-on-dia/)), and Logseq has split into a frozen Markdown edition and a beta database edition whose README warns of possible data loss ([Logseq README](https://github.com/logseq/logseq/)). Local-first storage insulates Mindstream from the hosted-service failure mode, and durable, exportable text is a selling point in a field where users worry about exactly this. The less comfortable half of this risk is epistemic: every "not found" above could be overturned by a single product the search missed or a primary page the network blocked, and the mechanics with no precedent are also the ones with no evidence that users want them.

## Conclusion

The comparison changes how Mindstream should describe itself. Its outer forms are familiar, which lowers the learning cost: a GTD user will read a parked context as a project on hold with a Waiting For action, an outliner user will read drill as zoom, and anyone who has used a share sheet will understand "Send to…". Its originality lies in a set of rules about the order of thinking, and three of those rules have outside support that the project can cite: the Rust RFC process for the four-way disposition of open questions, Leroy and Glomb for writing a resume note at the moment of interruption, and the CFAR experience for treating a sharper question as a result. The design-rationale literature supplies the constraint that should govern everything planned: structure is welcome after the writing and resented before it.

The practical implication is a sequencing one. The highest-value borrowings are small additions to the two tools that already exist, and they directly address the two risks most likely to hurt early use, an opaque gate and a context that is hard to re-enter. The planned shell can follow well-worn platform patterns with little invention. Before any novelty claim is made publicly, the unreachable primary sources deserve a direct read, starting with the Ink & Switch notes on prose versioning, the Amazing Marvin and Intend documentation, and official pages for the products this report had to leave unverified.
