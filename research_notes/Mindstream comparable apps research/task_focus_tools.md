# Task-execution, single-tasking and task-decomposition apps comparable to Mindstream's Focus runner

Research date: 24 November 2026. Method note for the report writer: this session's network blocked direct fetching of most vendor sites (intend.do, llamalife.co, blitzit.app, taskwarrior.org, nesslabs.com, help.amazingmarvin.com, lifedev.net all failed to resolve). Findings below therefore rest mainly on search-result summaries of those pages plus the one primary page that could be fetched (Super Productivity's GitHub README). Where a search summary came from a vendor's own page, the vendor URL is cited; where it came from third-party/affiliate reviews, that is flagged. Anything I know only from background knowledge and could not source in this session is listed under "Gaps" and explicitly labelled unverified. No pricing figure here was confirmed on an official pricing page.

## 1. One-thing-at-a-time execution views: how do apps present a single current task, and what sits alongside it?

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern is "one task + a timer (usually a countdown or Pomodoro) + a peek at what is next", fed from a pre-planned daily list; Llama Life, Amazing Marvin's Super Focus Mode, Sunsama's Focus mode, Tiimo, Routinery, Blitzit and Super Productivity all do a variant of it. None of the sources found describes a view that shows the current task together with its chain of parent goals (a stack/breadcrumb) or that lets you push a new, smaller focus on top of the current one mid-execution; that combination appears to be unoccupied.

### Cited Findings
- Llama Life describes itself as a daily task planner "designed to help you focus on ONE task at a time", with a countdown timer on every task (timeboxing), a schedule view showing total list time and estimated finishing time, and manual editing of time spent. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)
- Llama Life is on iOS and Android (and markets itself as an "ADHD Organizer"/"ADHD Routine & Task" app, i.e. it is still being re-titled and listed in 2026). — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750); [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.llamaapp)
- Llama Life is subscription-priced (monthly or annual) after a short free trial; listings disagree on trial length (3 days vs 7 days), and the only concrete prices surfaced were Swedish-store prices (79 kr/month, 499 kr/year). — [App Store SE](https://apps.apple.com/se/app/llama-life/id6454469750?l=en-GB); [App Store US](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)
- Capterra's Llama Life listing mentions visually grouped tasks and presets for recurring task lists. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1031154/llama-life)
- Amazing Marvin's Super Focus Mode (SFM) is an opt-in "strategy" enabled from the Features/Strategies menu, opened from the list's "..." menu or by pressing "F"; tasks appear one after another in the same top-to-bottom order as the daily list; if the time-tracking strategy is on, the timer starts automatically for the current task. An option "Always show current item in Super Focus Mode" interacts with the "Top of Mind" feature. — [Marvin Help: Super Focus Mode](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/2237484-super-focus-mode-sfm); [Marvin Help: Top of Mind](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/5364004-top-of-mind)
- In Marvin's focus mode, pressing the spacebar opens an input that sends a new item to the master list (capture without leaving focus). — [Amazing Marvin blog, "Dear Marvin"](https://amazingmarvin.com/blog/dear-marvin-teacher-with-adhd-and-too-much-to-do-needs-a-good-system/)
- Marvin frames focus as the end of a progressive narrowing "funnel": Master List -> Backburner -> Week -> Day -> Spotlight -> Focus Mode. — [Amazing Marvin for ADHD](https://amazingmarvin.com/for/adhd/); [The Marvin Method](https://amazingmarvin.com/features/funnel/); [Spotlight help article](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/13421851-spotlight)
- Sunsama's Focus mode is described by reviewers as a full-screen view that highlights the current task and hides the others; inside it you can add sub-tasks, take notes, and press Play to start a timer tracking work to the second; a Pomodoro-style timer is built in. (Third-party reviews, not Sunsama docs.) — [The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/some-first-impressions-of-sunsama-the-focus-course-in-app-form/); [Saner.ai review 2026](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-reviews); [Asian Efficiency review, updated 2026-07-06](https://www.asianefficiency.com/schedule-management/sunsama-review/)
- Sunsama pricing is reported inconsistently across 2026 reviews ($16, $20 and $25 per month); one review headline uses "$25/Month". Cloud SaaS. — [Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-reviews); [Upbase](https://upbase.io/blog/?p=4307)
- Tiimo's subtask timer view shows a completed step, a countdown for the current step, and the upcoming steps; the vendor says the visual timer "helps you smoothly transition between tasks". Tiimo was Apple's iPhone App of the Year 2025 and its listing carries a 2026 copyright. — [Tiimo visual planner page](https://www.tiimoapp.com/product/visual-planner); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/tiimo/id1480220328)
- Routinery runs a routine as an ordered sequence in which each step gets its own timer and the app walks you from one activity to the next, announcing by voice what is next; free tier reportedly 2 routines with ads, premium cited at $7.99/mo (sources are mostly Routinery's own blog and disagree on price). — [Routinery blog](https://www.routinery.app/blog/best-routine-apps-with-timer-and-progress); [Routinery blog 2](https://www.routinery.app/blog/apps-that-start-daily-routine-you-so-you-don-t-have-remember-each-task)
- Super Productivity's focus mode offers "Pomodoro, Flowtime and countdown sessions"; starting one on a task records time against it, and a break reminder prompts you to step away. It is offline-first ("Your data stays on your device unless you choose to sync it"), MIT-licensed, on Linux, macOS, Windows, Android, iOS and web, with optional sync via SuperSync (E2E encrypted, self-hostable), Dropbox or WebDAV. — [Super Productivity GitHub README](https://github.com/super-productivity/super-productivity)
- Blitzit keeps an "ever-visible countdown timer" as its main focus device and has a built-in review of work habits; integrates with Notion and Google Calendar; pricing reports conflict ($4.99 starting price, $6.99/mo, ~$44.91/year). One older article says desktop only (PC/Mac), newer listings show an iOS/iPad app. Product Hunt 4.1/5 from 15 reviews, with complaints when "core task data or access becomes unstable"; some review-site praise was vendor-solicited. — [Tom's Guide](https://www.tomsguide.com/computing/i-bought-this-productivity-app-from-instagram-and-its-now-an-essential-part-of-my-toolset); [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/10020211/Blitzit/); [Product Hunt reviews](https://www.producthunt.com/products/blitzit-2/reviews); [Software Advice](https://www.softwareadvice.com/product/519553-Blitzit/reviews/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/kr/app/id6743005253)
- Focusmate's single-task framing is social rather than visual: each 50-minute session starts with 10-30 seconds of declaring "the project you're working on, and the specific tasks/components you'll work on during the session", and at the bell partners report how it went. — [Focusmate help center](https://support.focusmate.com/en/articles/4044432-what-happens-during-a-focusmate-session-do-s-don-ts)
- Session (Pomodoro focus timer, Apple platforms) tracks "what you have worked on, what have you learned, and for how long"; a reviewer describes a post-session prompt to reflect and rate productivity. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/session-pomodoro-focus-timer/id1521432881); [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/products/session-1)
- Centered: the AppSumo listing states that "Centered app went bankrupt", that new owners took over the assets, and that the product was renamed. Treat Centered as discontinued/changed hands; date not confirmed. — [AppSumo Centered reviews](https://appsumo.com/products/centered/reviews/)
- Intend (formerly Complice, renamed after about nine years; founder Malcolm Ocean) has a Pomodoro timer with automatic and one-off modes, custom work/break lengths and desktop notifications, plus coworking rooms (the longest-running is the Less Wrong Study Hall). — [Intend features page](https://intend.do/features?from=complice.co); ["Complice has become Intend"](https://intentionality.substack.com/p/complice-is-now-intend)
- TickTick's Pomodoro timer attaches to individual tasks and tracks time spent on each; Todoist has no built-in timer. (Third-party comparisons.) — [Zapier TickTick vs Todoist](https://zapier.com/blog/ticktick-vs-todoist/); [Ellie comparison](https://ellieplanner.com/comparisons/todoist-vs-ticktick)
- Microsoft's Windows 11 Clock "Focus Sessions" reportedly gained To Do integration and end-of-session reflection labels (Deep focus, Focused, Steady, Drifting, Distracted), evidence that intention-plus-reflection is becoming a mainstream pattern. — [Windows Forum](https://windowsforum.com/threads/windows-11-clock-focus-sessions-gets-to-do-reflections-and-ai-hints.415694/)

### Inferences
- Almost every comparable is "list-first": you plan a queue, then the focus view pops the head of the queue. Mindstream's Focus runner is "focus-first" (type what you are doing now; structure emerges downward by decomposition). No source describes that inversion in a shipping app.
- Timers are overwhelmingly countdowns (timebox/Pomodoro). An elapsed (count-up) timer as the default, as in Mindstream, is the minority; Super Productivity's "Flowtime" mode and Sunsama's play-button tracking are the nearest. Countdown framing is what Llama Life and Routinery users are sold on ("timeboxing"), so an optional per-focus timebox could be a cheap adaptation, but it is not required to be comparable.
- Common "alongside" elements worth borrowing: next-up peek (Tiimo, Routinery), finish-time estimate (Llama Life), quick-capture hotkey that does not break focus (Marvin's spacebar), and an always-on-top mini timer (Blitzit).
- What nobody shows alongside the current task is the reason it exists (parent chain). Mindstream's breadcrumb is a genuine differentiator.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch llamalife.co, blitzit.app, intend.do or Marvin's help center directly, so exact layouts (e.g. whether Llama Life shows the next task, whether Blitzit's floating timer shows notes) are unconfirmed. Unverified background knowledge: Llama Life has a web app as well as mobile, shows confetti on completion and supports manually typed sub-tasks; Blitzit has an always-on-top floating "Blitz mode" widget with a "Blitz now" action; Marvin's SFM shows the single task with its subtasks and notes.
- Things 3, Todoist, Akiflow, Motion, Structured, Sukha/Flow Club: no sources were obtained in this session. Unverified background knowledge: Things 3 and Todoist have no dedicated single-task execution view (Things has a "Today" list; Todoist relies on third-party timers); Akiflow and Motion are calendar/time-blocking tools with a "focus mode" on the current block, Motion's scheduling being AI-driven; Structured is a manual day timeline with subtasks; Sukha (ex-Centered-like flow music + coach) and Flow Club are body-doubling services. None should be reported as fact without checking.
- "Now Then"-style apps: no reliable source found.
- Whether any app shows the parent goal next to the current task in its focus view: no source found either way; absence is an inference from the descriptions gathered.

## 2. Decomposition: which apps help break a task down in the moment, how deep, and AI vs manual?

### Takeaway
In-the-moment decomposition is now mostly sold as an AI feature (Goblin Tools Magic ToDo, Llama Life "Break it down", Tiimo AI checklists); manual decomposition exists everywhere as "add subtask" but is a planning gesture inside a list, not an execution gesture that changes what you are focused on. No source describes a manual "push a smaller focus onto a stack and return to the parent when done" mechanic.

### Cited Findings
- Goblin Tools Magic ToDo (AI): a "spiciness" slider controls granularity (low gives a handful of chunks, high can give a dozen-plus micro-steps); every generated item has its own button to edit, remove or break down further into subtasks, so nesting is recursive on demand; items can be drag-reordered; export to Todoist, iCalendar, a nested Markdown checklist, or a proprietary .goblin file; accounts now add history and cross-device sync. Free on the web; paid mobile apps. — [Goblin Tools Android web](https://android.goblin.tools/ToDo); [Gigazine hands-on, March 2025](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250326-magic-todo); [DataStudios explainer](https://www.datastudios.org/post/goblin-tools-magic-todo-s-task-breakdown-mechanism-and-pricing-explained); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6449003064)
- Sentiment on Goblin Tools: Saner.ai's 2026 review rates breakdown quality 4.5/5 and the tool 3.9/5 overall, noting stability complaints and generic output on repeated use; a university library recommends it for neurodivergent users. — [Saner.ai Goblin Tools review 2026](https://blog.saner.ai/goblin-tools-review/); [University of Warwick Library](https://warwick.ac.uk/services/library/using/productivity-tools/magic-todo-gobin-tools)
- Llama Life (AI): "Break it down" in the Task Actions menu uses AI to split a task into smaller steps and suggests an estimated time per step. — [App Store listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/adhd-organizer-llama-life/id6454469750)
- Tiimo (AI): "smart checklists use AI to quickly break down your tasks into simple steps"; "A single prompt turns into a structured plan." The resulting steps then run in the step-by-step timer view. — [Tiimo AI planner page](https://tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/ai-planner); [Tiimo site](https://www.tiimoapp.com)
- Sunsama (manual): subtasks can be added from inside Focus mode, i.e. decomposition during execution is possible, but as a flat checklist under the task. — [The Sweet Setup](https://thesweetsetup.com/some-first-impressions-of-sunsama-the-focus-course-in-app-form/)
- Amazing Marvin (manual): users praise "the ease of creating Sub-Tasks within Tasks/Projects"; it is known for extreme configurability with a learning-curve cost (the headline complaint in 2026 reviews). — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/238806/Amazing-Marvin/); [Saner.ai Marvin review 2026](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/)
- Super Productivity (manual): tasks are organised with sub-tasks, projects and tags; nesting depth is not stated in the README. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/super-productivity/super-productivity)
- WorkFlowy (manual): outline format with infinite nesting, contrasted with Todoist's flatter projects. — [Breeze, Todoist alternatives 2026](https://www.breeze.pm/articles/todoist-alternatives)
- OmniFocus (manual): later actions in a sequential project are blocked until earlier ones complete, so only the next action shows as available; this is the classic way an outline is turned into a one-at-a-time stream. — [Omni forum: filter blocked/waiting](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/filter-blocked-waiting-for-in-perspective/71139); [OmniFocus 4 Reference Manual, Perspectives](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/perspectives/)
- Taskade positions itself around AI agents that generate tasks/apps from a prompt (vendor's own comparison page). — [Taskade comparison page](https://www.taskade.com/compare/free-ticktick-alternative)

### Inferences
- The market has converged on "the AI breaks it down for you". Mindstream's stance (the human decomposes, by naming the next smaller thing) is counter-positioned, and the stack mechanic makes manual decomposition cheap: one typed line, no list management.
- Goblin Tools' most borrowable non-AI mechanic is the per-item "break this one down further" affordance: decomposition is offered at the exact item that feels too big, not as a planning mode. Mindstream already does this for the current card; it could extend it to pre-listing several sibling sub-foci at once (a small queue under the current focus), since AI tools always produce multiple steps while the Focus runner produces one at a time.
- Per-step time estimates (Llama Life, Tiimo) are tied to countdown timers; they are optional for Mindstream.
- Outliners (WorkFlowy, OmniFocus action groups) give unlimited depth but show the whole tree; Mindstream's "only the top of the stack is visible" is the differentiating constraint.

### Gaps
- Nesting depth limits were not confirmed for any app. Unverified background knowledge: Todoist allows up to 4 indent levels of sub-tasks; Things 3 has only projects > headings > to-dos > flat checklist items; TickTick allows about 5 levels; OmniFocus action groups and WorkFlowy/Checkvist/Taskade outlines are effectively unlimited; Amazing Marvin tasks have one level of subtasks but projects/sub-projects nest arbitrarily; Llama Life has one level of manual sub-tasks; Super Productivity has one level of sub-tasks. Verify before publishing.
- WorkFlowy "zoom" with breadcrumb (the closest visual analogue to Mindstream's stack + breadcrumb) could not be sourced in this session; it is a long-standing feature to my knowledge but unverified here.
- No ADHD-forum (r/ADHD, r/productivity) threads were retrieved, so user sentiment on AI vs manual breakdown is limited to review sites.

## 3. Recording outcomes: does any app ask for a takeaway on completion and roll it up to the parent?

### Takeaway
No app found asks for a one-line takeaway when a sub-task is completed and attaches it to the parent task. The nearest analogues work at a different grain: per focus session (Session, Focusmate, Windows Focus Sessions: "how did it go?"), per day (Sunsama shutdown), per goal per period (Intend's review system), or as a free-form timestamped log (interstitial journaling).

### Cited Findings
- Intend/Complice: each day starts fresh and you set intentions "based on what feels energizing, important and feasible that day"; in Complice each intention had to be tied to one of your (numbered) goals or explicitly marked miscellaneous; the to-do list resets every day unless you consciously carry intentions over. — [Intend home](https://intend.do/?from=complice.co); [Coleman Foley's Complice review](https://colemanfoley.medium.com/complice-review-eac7aaf6bd45); [LifeDev, "6 Months with Complice" (2018)](https://lifedev.net/2018/02/15/complice-review/)
- Intend's review feature is "a unified space to complete weekly, monthly and quarterly reviews" with pre-loaded questions and custom questions per goal; the founder calls it a "fractal review system that synthesizes daily/weekly/monthly/quarterly/yearly reflections on each of your long-term goals or life areas". This is the clearest existing example of lower-level outcomes being rolled up into a higher-level record. — [Intend features](https://intend.do/features?from=complice.co); [Malcolm Ocean blog](https://malcolmocean.com/2022/11/my-resistances-to-growing-complice/)
- Intend is a small, founder-run web app still operating (a business write-up puts it at about $23K MRR); a 2018 review praised its cohesion ("the whole is more than the sum of its parts") and criticised the utilitarian design. — [Superframeworks](https://superframeworks.com/blog/intend); [LifeDev](https://lifedev.net/2018/02/15/complice-review/)
- Sunsama: an evening guided shutdown has you "review what you accomplished, carry forward what you didn't, and disconnect from work"; tasks also carry notes, comments and an activity log. (Third-party review.) — [Saner.ai Sunsama review](https://blog.saner.ai/sunsama-reviews); [ClickUp's Sunsama review](https://clickup.com/blog/sunsama-review/)
- Session app records "what have you learned" per focus session and prompts for a reflection/productivity rating afterwards. — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/gb/app/session-pomodoro-focus-timer/id1521432881)
- Focusmate: at the end bell partners "report on how the session went". — [Focusmate help](https://support.focusmate.com/en/articles/4044432-what-happens-during-a-focusmate-session-do-s-don-ts); [Daily.co case study](https://www.daily.co/blog/how-focusmate-helps-people-be-their-best-selves-building-on-dailys-prebuilt-ui/)
- Super Productivity's last daily step is to review completed work and export it as timesheets/work summaries or worklogs to Jira/GitLab/OpenProject/Redmine: an outcome roll-up, but of time, not of learnings. — [GitHub README](https://github.com/super-productivity/super-productivity)
- Interstitial journaling: credited to Tony Stubblebine's 2017 article "Replace Your To-Do List With Interstitial Journaling To Increase Productivity"; the practice is to write a few timestamped lines at every task switch or break; Ness Labs (Anne-Laure Le Cunff) popularised it as "combining notes, to-do and time tracking". — [Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/interstitial-journaling); [awesome-time-tracking entry](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ever-works/awesome-time-tracking/develop/details/interstitial-journaling.md); [Why is this interesting?](https://whyisthisinteresting.substack.com/p/interstitial-journaling-edition)
- A blog claim that writing between tasks "improves performance by 23%" (attributed to a Harvard study) could not be verified and should not be repeated. — [mylifenote blog](https://blog.mylifenote.ai/interstitial-journaling.md)

### Inferences
- Mindstream's takeaway-on-finish is effectively structured interstitial journaling: the journal entry is prompted at exactly the switch point (finishing a sub-focus), kept to one line, and filed on the parent instead of in a chronological log. That framing is accurate and defensible.
- Intend's fractal reviews suggest an extension: when a parent focus is finished, show the takeaways collected from its children as the raw material for the parent's own takeaway (roll-up by re-summarising, level by level).
- Session/Windows-style quick ratings ("Deep focus ... Distracted") are a lighter alternative to text but record feeling, not result; they do not serve Mindstream's goal of carrying results upward.
- Since the takeaway is optional, the stopping moment is also the natural place for a "where I left off / next step" line when a focus is abandoned or parked rather than finished (see section 5).

### Gaps
- Could not fetch intend.do or the reviews directly, so the exact daily "outcomes" mechanic is unsourced. Unverified background knowledge: in Intend/Complice you end the day by "submitting outcomes", marking each intention done/not done and optionally annotating it, and the weekly review displays those outcomes per goal.
- Amazing Marvin: no source found for completion-time prompts. A third-party review claims Marvin lacks a dedicated notes space separate from tasks ([Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/)), unverified against Marvin docs.
- Logseq/Roam journal-based task workflows were not researched (partly out of scope as outliners); no claims made.
- No evidence was found, in either direction, of any app attaching a child's completion note to its parent automatically; "none exists" is an inference from the absence of results across roughly a dozen products, not a proven negative.

## 4. Parking/blocked work: how do GTD-style and tracker tools implement "Waiting For"?

### Takeaway
Three mechanisms exist: (a) a tag/list that removes the item from "available" views (OmniFocus On Hold tag, Things tag, Nirvana and FacileThings Waiting For lists); (b) a date that hides the item until it resurfaces (Taskwarrior `wait:`, FacileThings follow-up dates); (c) a dependency link that unblocks automatically when the blocker completes (Taskwarrior `depends:`, Marvin dependencies, Linear "blocked by"). All operate on single tasks; parking an entire working context with a free-text reason, as Mindstream does, has no direct counterpart found.

### Cited Findings
- OmniFocus 4: a tag can be set to On Hold; it "remains visible in the Sidebar and if you've chosen to show only Available items ... its assigned actions are hidden in the Outline". Users commonly make a "Waiting" tag On Hold so those actions drop out of Available, then build a custom perspective (filtered by tag/status, often "Remaining") to list them; others keep the tag Active on the grounds that waiting is an active state. Review guidance asks whether a project should be put On Hold or Dropped. — [OmniFocus 4 Reference Manual](https://support.omnigroup.com/documentation/omnifocus/universal/4.10.1/en/perspectives/); [Omni forum: 'Waiting' perspective](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/waiting-perspective/60399); [Omni forum: OF4 perspective for On Hold items](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/of4-a-perspective-for-on-hold-items/70460); [Omni forum: uses of tag status](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/what-are-the-uses-of-tag-status/53078); [Omni forum: how do you handle waiting](https://discourse.omnigroup.com/t/how-do-you-handle-waiting-for-responses-or-work-from-people/31048)
- Things 3 has no Waiting state; users assign a "WAITING FOR" tag and click it to list such items, or create a "waiting for ..." to-do as a project's next action to get a cross-project view for the weekly review. Things' nested tags are organisational only (parent tag not applied), unlike OmniFocus where tag hierarchy is respected. — [GTD forum: Things 3 and GTD](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/goto/post?id=111662); [MacDrifter](https://www.macdrifter.com/2018/02/things-3-from-an-omnifocus-and-taskpaper-user.html)
- Nirvana has a native Waiting For list that tracks who you are waiting on without cluttering your own action list; whether reminders fire for those items is unclear (app-store reviews say reminders are missing; a forum post says dated items push-notify). — [toolguide.io Nirvana](https://toolguide.io/en/tool/nirvana-2/); [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1022913190?l=en-us); [GTD forum: Nirvana HQ](https://forum.gettingthingsdone.com/threads/nirvana-hq.14939/page-12)
- FacileThings: Waiting For (delegated) items carry editable dates usable as follow-up reminders; a `:day` inline command adds a reminder; non-deadline dates act as reminders and do not appear on the calendar. — [FacileThings blog: deadlines and expected dates](https://facilethings.com/blog/en/deadlines-and-expected-dates); [FacileThings inline commands tutorial](https://facilethings.com/learning/en/tutorial/inline-commands); [FacileThings changelog Nov 2014](https://facilethings.com/blog/en/changelog-nov-2014)
- Taskwarrior: `wait:<date>` hides a task until the date arrives (e.g. `wait:due-7d`); hidden tasks are listed by `task waiting` ("Waiting (hidden) tasks") and carry the WAITING virtual tag; `depends:` lists tasks that must be completed first; reports `blocked`, `blocking` and `unblocked` list tasks blocked by others, tasks blocking others, and unblocked tasks. Local-first, free/open-source CLI. — [Taskwarrior: Using Dates Effectively](https://taskwarrior.org/docs/using_dates/); [Taskwarrior reports](https://taskwarrior.org/docs/report/); [Taskwarrior commands](https://taskwarrior.org/docs/commands/); [task(1) man page](https://manpages.ubuntu.com/manpages/jammy/man1/task.1.html)
- Amazing Marvin: a "dependency" strategy hides dependent items until prerequisites are done; when an item becomes available Marvin moves it from the Backburner to the Master List with a "new" tag, and hovering the tag shows why it became available. (Help article last updated years ago.) A blogger also describes a Review Date that can be put on any task or project with an alert. — [Marvin Help: Dependencies](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/2628279-dependencies); [Mac Automation Tips](https://macautomationtips.com/the-amazing-marvin-desktop-app-features-and-setup/)
- Amazing Marvin pricing: about $12/month or $96/year, 14-day trial, no free plan; the former ~$300 lifetime licence is reported as withdrawn by one source and still listed by another. Cloud-synced web/desktop/mobile. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/238806/Amazing-Marvin/); [Lemtask review](https://lemtask.com/reviews/amazing-marvin); [Saner.ai](https://blog.saner.ai/amazing-marvin-review/)
- Linear: blocking issues appear in the sidebar "with an orange flag under Blocked by"; "once the blocking issue has been resolved, the blocked issue flag turns green and moves under Related", and history entries are written on both issues when the blocker is resolved or reopened. — [Linear docs: issue relations](https://linear.app/docs/issue-relations); [Linear changelog 2020-03-11](https://linear.app/changelog/2020-03-11-issue-relations)
- Jira: the flag is a "Flagged" checkbox with the single value Impediment; flagging does not change status (the card turns light yellow with a flag icon and stays in place) and must be removed manually; teams debate flag vs a dedicated Blocked status vs an "is blocked by" link. — [Atlassian: Flagging an issue](https://confluence.atlassian.com/display/JIRASOFTWARECLOUD/Flagging+an+issue); [Atlassian Community: Why flagging is so cool](https://community.atlassian.com/t5/Jira-articles/Why-flagging-Jira-issues-is-so-cool/ba-p/1872469); [Atlassian Community: flag vs status](https://community.atlassian.com/forums/Jira-questions/should-blocked-be-a-status-or-some-kind-of-atttribure/qaq-p/1111889)

### Inferences
- The weak point of pure tag/list approaches (Things, OmniFocus tags) is resurfacing: the item returns only if the user runs a review. Tools that fix this add either a date (Taskwarrior `wait`, FacileThings, Marvin review date) or an auto-unblock event (Linear, Marvin dependencies, Taskwarrior `depends`).
- Borrowable mechanics for Mindstream's PARK, in increasing cost: (1) show the "waiting on ..." text and elapsed parked time directly on the context in the side rail (Linear's orange flag; GTD's "who + since when" convention); (2) an optional "check back on" date that visually nudges the parked context when it passes (Taskwarrior/FacileThings); (3) a visible "why this came back" hint when it resurfaces (Marvin's "new" tag with reason on hover); (4) "waiting on another context" that auto-clears when that context finishes (Linear green flag) is probably overkill for single-user use.
- Jira's community debate (flag vs status) maps to a design choice: Mindstream's park is a flag on the context that preserves its stack position, not a move to another list. That matches the Jira-flag philosophy (state preserved, marker added) and is the right fit for "resume exactly where I left off".
- A parked context is conceptually closer to GTD's "project on hold with a Waiting For next action" than to a waiting task; describing it that way will be familiar to GTD users.

### Gaps
- No Amazing Marvin "Waiting for" strategy documentation was found in two searches; only "Dependencies" and "Review Date" surfaced. Unverified background knowledge: Marvin does have a "Waiting for" strategy that lets you mark an item as waiting with a note, moves it to a waiting list and reminds you on a follow-up date. Verify on Marvin's help center (blocked in this session).
- Todoist: no source obtained. Unverified background knowledge: it has no native waiting state; users use a `@waiting` label plus a filter.
- OmniFocus defer dates as a resurfacing mechanism (hide until date) were not confirmed by an official source here, though they are a core, long-standing feature to my knowledge.
- No user-sentiment data (complaints/praise) specifically about waiting-for features beyond the forum workflow descriptions cited.

## 5. Context switching between separate problems and resuming where you left off

### Takeaway
The strongest research backing is Leroy and Glomb's "ready-to-resume plan": about a minute spent noting where you stopped and what you will do on return reduces attention residue and protects performance on the next task. Products mostly invoke this research rhetorically (interstitial journaling posts); no task app found has a first-class "saved context with resume note" feature comparable to Mindstream's parked contexts in a side rail.

### Cited Findings
- Leroy & Glomb (2018), "Tasks Interrupted: How Anticipating Time Pressure on Resumption of an Interrupted Task Causes Attention Residue and Low Performance on Interrupting Tasks and How a 'Ready-to-Resume' Plan Mitigates the Effects", Organization Science 29(3):380-397. — [EconPapers record](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:inm:ororsc:v:29:y:2018:i:3:p:380-397); [IDEAS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/inm/ororsc/v29y2018i3p380-397.html)
- Findings as reported by the University of Washington: people who expect to resume an interrupted task under time pressure struggle to switch attention and perform worse on the interrupting task; briefly writing a plan for the return (where you stopped, what comes next; about a minute) reduced residue and protected performance. Evidence: a field survey of 202 professionals plus lab studies (studies 3 and 4 had 66 and 44 student participants). Limitation: the studies did not test whether the plan improves performance on the original task after returning to it. — [UW News, 16 Jan 2018](https://www.washington.edu/news/2018/01/16/task-interrupted-a-plan-for-returning-helps-you-move-on/); [ScienceDaily](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2018/01/180116151600.htm)
- A secondary claim that plan-writers were "79% more likely" to choose the best candidate in the résumé task is unverified against the paper. — [Lawyerist](https://lawyerist.com/news/combatting-attention-residue-when-faced-with-interruptions/)
- Interstitial-journaling write-ups tie the practice to Sophie Leroy's attention/task-residue research (attention lingers on the previous task); sources disagree on her affiliation at the time (University of Washington vs UW Bothell; the original attention-residue paper dates to 2009). Suggested entry formats include "timestamp, feeling, next task". — [beehiiv newsletter](https://motivated-and-miffed.beehiiv.com/p/your-attention-doesn-t-switch-it-drags); [awesome-time-tracking: interstitial journaling practice](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/ever-works/awesome-time-tracking/develop/details/interstitial-journaling-practice.md); [Ness Labs](https://nesslabs.com/interstitial-journaling)
- Product-level context features found are adjacent only: Marvin's "Top of Mind" keeps the current item persistently visible; Super Productivity lets you "attach context information to tasks and projects" (notes, files, project-level bookmarks); Sunsama keeps an activity log per task. — [Marvin Help: Top of Mind](https://help.amazingmarvin.com/en/articles/5364004-top-of-mind); [Super Productivity README](https://github.com/super-productivity/super-productivity); [ClickUp's Sunsama review](https://clickup.com/blog/sunsama-review/)

### Inferences
- Mindstream's PARK with a "waiting on ..." reason is half of a ready-to-resume plan (why it stopped). The research supports adding the other half at the moment of switching: an optional one-line "when I come back, first I will ..." note, shown prominently when the context is resumed. This is the single most evidence-backed borrowable mechanic in this research, and it also applies when switching contexts without parking.
- Mindstream's stack plus breadcrumb plus accumulated takeaways already amounts to an automatic resume state ("where am I, how did I get here, what have I learned so far"); surfacing the last takeaway and time-since-last-touched on resume would complete the picture.
- Be careful with claims: Leroy and Glomb showed benefit for the interrupting task, not proven benefit for resumption quality; the Zeigarnik effect is frequently cited by productivity products but no primary or product source for it was gathered here.

### Gaps
- No product page citing the Zeigarnik effect was retrieved; do not attribute Zeigarnik claims to specific products on the basis of these notes.
- No sources were found on dedicated "saved workspace/session" features in task apps (window/tab session managers and developer tools were out of scope and not searched).
- Leroy's original 2009 attention-residue paper was not directly sourced; only secondary mentions.

## 6. Runnable checklists/procedures executed one step at a time for personal use

### Takeaway
Step-at-a-time runners for individuals exist almost only as timed habit routines (Routinery, Tiimo), where steps are prompts with countdowns and capture nothing. Checklist-run products that capture data per step (Process Street, Manifestly, Tallyfy) are team/business SaaS priced per user. A personal runner whose steps each produce text or an action, with a hand-off to a writing tool and the result brought back, was not found.

### Cited Findings
- Routinery: a routine is an ordered sequence with a duration on every step and repeat days; each step runs on its own timer and the app advances and announces the next step by voice, hands-free. Its blog claims it is the only app in its comparison "whose store listing runs a routine as an ordered sequence with a timer on each step" (vendor claim). It also now markets AI-generated routines. — [Routinery blog: timers and progress](https://www.routinery.app/blog/best-routine-apps-with-timer-and-progress); [Routinery blog: turn daily tasks into a routine](https://www.routinery.app/blog/how-to-turn-daily-tasks-into-a-routine); [Routinery blog: AI routine habit](https://www.routinery.app/blog/ai-routine-habit)
- Routinery's blog contrasts itself with list apps: "Todoist, Microsoft To Do and Things 3 will store the tasks but leave the sequencing to you." — [Routinery blog](https://www.routinery.app/blog/best-task-scheduling-to-do-routine-apps)
- Tiimo: routines and checklists run with a visual countdown per step showing done / current / upcoming. — [Tiimo visual planner](https://www.tiimoapp.com/product/visual-planner)
- Structured is characterised as a clean, manual planner built around a daily timeline. — [Tiimo AI planner resource](https://tiimoapp.com/resource-hub/ai-planner)
- Llama Life offers presets for recurring task lists, which then run through its one-task countdown view. — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.co.uk/software/1031154/llama-life)
- Process Street, Manifestly and Tallyfy are business workflow/checklist products: Process Street emphasises conditional logic, compliance and document control; Manifestly is a focused recurring-checklist product from about $10/user/month; Tallyfy distinguishes "builder" and "completer" seat types. All sources are vendor comparison pages aimed at teams; none addresses single-user personal use. — [Process Street vs Tallyfy (vendor)](https://www.process.st/process-street-vs-tallyfy/); [Process Street: Manifestly alternatives (vendor)](https://www.process.st/manifestly-alternatives/); [Manifestly: Process Street alternative (vendor)](https://manifest.ly/process-street-alternative); [G2 comparison](https://www.g2.com/compare/manifestly-checklists-vs-process-street)

### Inferences
- Two existing models bracket Mindstream's planned "workflow" feature: (a) routine runners: one step on screen, next step peek, progress indicator, but no captured output; (b) process runners: a template is instantiated as a "run", and steps can hold form fields whose values persist with that run. Mindstream's planned design (each step produces text or an action, kept per run) is model (b) with model (a)'s single-step presentation, for one user, locally.
- Borrowable mechanics: the template-vs-run distinction (edit the workflow definition separately from an execution of it, keep past runs as records); done / current / upcoming step strip (Tiimo); letting a step be skipped with a reason; carrying an earlier step's answer forward as visible context for later steps.
- The "open a writing tool from a step and bring the result back" hand-off has no analogue in any source found; the nearest general pattern is a form field in a process run, which is typed in place.
- In the Focus runner's terms, a workflow step is just a pre-authored sub-focus and its captured text is its takeaway; this suggests workflows and the focus stack can share one mechanism.

### Gaps
- Process Street's run UI was not confirmed from documentation. Unverified background knowledge: a run shows the task list on the left and the selected task's content and form fields (text, dropdown, date, file) on the right; tasks can be forced in order ("stop tasks"); earlier answers can be inserted into later steps as variables. Verify before citing.
- Checkvist: two searches returned nothing. Unverified background knowledge: Checkvist is a keyboard-driven outliner with unlimited nesting and list duplication for repeatable checklists; no dedicated one-step run mode.
- Amazing Marvin "procedures": no such feature name was found; Marvin's nearest features to my knowledge are task/project templates and recurring tasks (unverified). The assignment's term may not correspond to a real Marvin feature.
- Apple Shortcuts-style personal runbooks and flight-checklist-inspired apps: not researched within the tool budget; no claims made.
- Whether any personal checklist app lets a step launch another tool and return a result: no evidence found; treat as "no comparable feature identified", not as proven absent.
