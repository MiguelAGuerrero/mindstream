# Tools-for-thought and writing apps comparable to Mindstream's ThinkPad

Research date: 2026-11-24. Method note for the report writer: findings below come from web search result summaries; direct page fetches failed in this session (notes.andymatuschak.org, inkandswitch.com and allume.com were unreachable from the sandbox network), so quotes are as surfaced by search, not read in full on the page. Anything I know only from background knowledge and could not source in this session is placed under "Inferences" or "Gaps" and labelled as unverified. Pricing figures come mostly from aggregator sites that disagree with each other; treat every price as approximate.

Mapping of ThinkPad mechanics to sections: (1) Stream and (2) stamp/freeze -> "Free-write then refine"; (3) tag and drill -> "Zoom/hoist" and "Lightweight annotation"; (4) resolve/replace span, staged versions -> "Fold-back / roll-up" and the versions part of "Free-write then refine"; (5) open-item gate -> "Convergence or completeness gate".

## Zoom / hoist / drill-into-a-node while keeping context

### Takeaway
Drilling into one node with a breadcrumb trail is a mature, standard pattern (Workflowy, Org-mode, Logseq/Roam, Heptabase sub-whiteboards, Muse nested boards), and the usual context-keeping devices are breadcrumbs, a second pane, and side-by-side stacked columns. What none of the sourced tools does is open the node as a *blank* page: every one of them zooms into the existing content of the node, so ThinkPad's "blank sub-pad plus goal line plus peek at parent" is a different contract from outliner zoom.

### Cited Findings
- Workflowy: zooming into an item creates a breadcrumb trail; any ancestor on the path is clickable; when the path is too long it collapses into an ellipsis ("···"). Keyboard: zoom in Alt/Cmd+., zoom out Alt/Cmd+,, home Ctrl/Cmd+'. — [Workflowy sidebar & navigation help](https://workflowy.com/help/sidebar-navigation); [Workflowy keyboard shortcuts](https://workflowy.com/learn/keyboard-shortcuts)
- Workflowy panes: "A pane is another view of an existing item, not a duplicate. Each pane has its own breadcrumbs, search, back and forward history, scroll position, and page controls." This is the closest sourced analogue to "peek at parent" (parent in one pane, child in another). — [Workflowy panes help](https://workflowy.com/help/panes)
- Org-mode offers two distinct mechanisms: `org-narrow-to-subtree` (C-x n s) narrows the current buffer to one subtree and `widen` restores it; `org-tree-to-indirect-buffer` (C-c C-x b) shows the subtree in an indirect buffer which "contains the entire buffer, but is narrowed to the current tree. Editing the indirect buffer also changes the original buffer, but without affecting visibility in that buffer." — [Org manual, Global and local cycling](https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/manual/html_node/org/Global-and-local-cycling.html); discussion of the trade-off in [Org mailing list "Narrowing vs indirect buffer"](https://list.orgmode.org/874n4slsks.fsf@bzg.ath.cx/T/)
- Org-mode users reported (2012-era threads) that edits made in an indirect buffer can land in the wrong place in the original buffer; old reports, current status unverified. — [Org mailing list "Local subtree view"](https://list.orgmode.org/8662azcppy.fsf@sachachua.com/T/)
- Heptabase: "When you have many cards on a whiteboard, you can create sub-whiteboards for researching subtopics under the main topic"; created by right-clicking empty canvas and choosing "Whiteboard". The public roadmap lists a "Sub-whiteboard preview mode" in v1.116.0 (November 2026), i.e. a peek at the child without entering it, shipped very recently. — [Heptabase wiki: Nested whiteboard](https://wiki.heptabase.com/nested-whiteboard); [Heptabase public roadmap](https://wiki.heptabase.com/roadmap)
- Muse: nested boards "add layers to your thinking" (boards inside boards, navigated by zooming). — [Setapp listing for Muse](https://setapp.com/apps/628)
- Andy Matuschak-style stacked/sliding panes: the Obsidian "Sliding Panes (Andy Matuschak Mode)" plugin author says he was inspired by how Matuschak's notes site opens linked notes in columns "so you could see where you'd been"; users open several panes and scroll horizontally between them. — [Obsidian forum: Sliding Panes plugin](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/sliding-panes-andy-matushcak-mode-plugin/7652/54); [plugin author's portfolio page](https://www.death.id.au/cv.05/)
- A 2023 Obsidian forum thread is titled "Sliding panes missing", which indicates the community plugin stopped working or was superseded at some point (Obsidian later shipped stacked tabs natively; see Inferences). — [Obsidian forum: Sliding panes missing](https://forum.obsidian.md/t/sliding-panes-missing/75450)
- Obsidian has no native outliner zoom; it is supplied by community plugins such as "Zoom into section" and "Workflowy-style outline". — [Obsidian community plugin: zoom-into-section](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/zoom-into-section); [workflowy-style-outline](https://community.obsidian.md/plugins/workflowy-style-outline)
- Roam added the ability, when embedding a block, to also embed the path to that block "to preserve context"; a Logseq user asked for the same. This is breadcrumb-as-context applied to transcluded content. — [Logseq forum: Can Logseq track embed paths like Roam Research](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/can-logseq-track-embed-paths-like-roam-research/10116)
- "Lost in hyperspace": Conklin (1987) defined disorientation as "the tendency to lose one's sense of location and direction in a non-linear document" and named disorientation and cognitive overhead as the two main hypertext problems; cognitive overhead is "the additional effort and concentration" needed "to maintain several tasks or trails" at once. — [Conklin 1987 (PDF copy)](https://www.ics.uci.edu/~andre/informatics223s2017/conklin.pdf); [The Electronic Labyrinth](https://www2.iath.virginia.edu/elab/hfl0097.html)
- Classic mitigations: graphical browsers / overview maps giving a 2-D view of the network while showing only a small part of it; maps or overviews to monitor progress; guided paths that "narrow down" choices, reducing both disorientation and cognitive overhead. — [Hypertext review, ch. 4](https://ps.uni-saarland.de/~duchier/pub/hypertext_review/chapter4.html)
- Counter-view: at least one paper argues the disorientation problem "has been greatly exaggerated", and another that solving it "goes beyond providing more and more navigational aids". — [McDonald, Applied Ergonomics 1996 (PDF)](https://www.interruptions.net/literature/McDonald-ApplErgon96.pdf); [Theng, J.UCS 4(11)](https://www.jucs.org/jucs_4_11/addressing_design_and_usability/Theng_Y_L.html)

### Inferences
- ThinkPad's drill is strictly hierarchical (a tree, one parent per sub-pad), which is the benign case for disorientation: the classic "lost in hyperspace" literature is about arbitrary link networks. A breadcrumb fully describes position in a tree, so breadcrumb + goal line directly addresses Conklin's two problems (location, and the overhead of remembering why you are here). The "goal reminder" has no sourced equivalent in the outliners above; it targets cognitive overhead rather than location.
- Borrowable: Workflowy's breadcrumb truncation with an ellipsis for deep nesting; Workflowy's per-pane independent history (lets the parent stay open beside the child rather than a modal peek); Org's indirect-buffer model (parent visibility state unaffected by what you do in the child); Heptabase's brand-new preview mode (hover/peek into a child from the parent, the inverse of ThinkPad's peek-at-parent).
- Stacked panes (Matuschak) solve "where have I been" by keeping the trail physically on screen. For a depth-N drill this is a natural alternative to a text breadcrumb: each ancestor a narrow column, the frozen parent visible to the left. Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Obsidian has shipped native "stacked tabs" since v1.0 (2022), which is probably why the plugin thread says "missing".
- Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Roam, Logseq, Tana and Dynalist all zoom into a bullet with a breadcrumb above it; Roam and Logseq open a block in a right sidebar via shift-click; Notion opens database pages in "side peek" or "center peek" and shows a page-path breadcrumb in the top bar; Bike has "Focus In/Out" on a row; Capacities and Scrintal open objects/cards side by side. The report writer should treat these as likely true but unverified here.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve official docs for zoom/breadcrumb behaviour in Roam, Logseq, Tana, Dynalist, Bike, Notion (side peek), Capacities or Scrintal; searches returned unrelated or thin results and page fetches were blocked.
- Andy Matuschak's own notes (notes.andymatuschak.org) could not be fetched, so no first-hand statement of his rationale for stacked panes is cited; the only sourced rationale is second-hand from the plugin author.
- No source found for user complaints specifically about getting lost in deep outliner zoom (as opposed to hypertext in general). I found no evidence either way.
- No tool was found that opens a drilled node as an empty page with the source paragraph held as a prompt. Absence of evidence only; the search was not exhaustive.

## Fold-back / roll-up: child work summarized or substituted into the parent

### Takeaway
Existing tools offer live transclusion (the same content shown in two places) or one-off flattening of a reference into text; none of the sourced tools has "the converged result of a child workspace replaces the original span in the parent, with the working-out archived". The nearest primitives are Roam's "replace reference with text" and mirrors/synced blocks, and they work in the opposite spirit: they keep parent and child identical rather than letting the child distil down to a replacement.

### Cited Findings
- Workflowy mirrors: "Mirrors in Workflowy let you display the same bullet in multiple places - and keep them perfectly in sync. When you edit a mirrored bullet, the changes show up everywhere it's mirrored." Mirrors get a diamond bullet; shortcut Alt/Cmd+Shift+M; the item menu lists all locations of a mirror; if only two mirrors exist and one is deleted, the survivor reverts to a regular bullet. — [Workflowy mirrors help](https://workflowy.com/learn/mirrors); [Workflowy blog: Mirrors](https://blog.workflowy.com/mirrors-create-live-copies-of-any-bullet/)
- Workflowy added "Mirror To" in Feb 2024 (send a mirror of a bullet to another location). — [Workflowy product update Feb 2024](https://blog.workflowy.com/product-update-feb-2024/)
- Roam block references can be converted in place to: an embed (transclusion; edits change the original), plain text (a detached copy; editing does not affect the original), an alias, or text + alias. "Replace with text" is the closest sourced operation to "result is substituted into the parent", but it copies the whole referenced block as it stands; nothing condenses or gates it. — [Outliner Software forum: Block Reference Improvements in Roam](https://www.outlinersoftware.com/topics/viewt/9127)
- Logseq: a block reference is described as "a window" onto another part of the graph, an embed as "a portal" through which the source can be edited directly; syntax `((uuid))` vs `{{embed ((uuid))}}`, the embed including children; the source block shows a reference counter. — [Logseq forum: block embeds vs block references](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/the-difference-between-logseqs-block-embeds-and-block-references/8459); [Logseq forum: basics of block references](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/the-basics-of-logseq-block-references/8458/3)
- Logseq users have debated whether block references/embeds are a "killer feature" and raised "logical problems with block reference"; i.e. the model is contested, not universally loved. — [Logseq forum: killer feature?](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/is-the-ability-to-reference-embed-a-block-a-killer-feature/8002); [Logseq forum: Logical problems with block reference](https://discuss.logseq.com/t/logical-problems-with-block-reference/4708)
- Notion synced blocks: "synced copies of blocks. A change in one location will be reflected everywhere that that block is synced"; the API distinguishes an original from duplicates. — [Notion Academy: Synced blocks](https://notion.com/help/notion-academy/lesson/synced-blocks); [Notion API block reference](https://developers.notion.com/reference/block)
- Notion toggles hide working-out under a heading; a side effect noted by a Notion educator is that text inside collapsed toggles is excluded from the page word count. — [Thomas Frank: Notion toggles](https://thomasjfrank.com/learn-notion/toggles/)
- Heptabase's nested whiteboards are pitched around "reusable cards": the same card appears on parent and sub-whiteboards, again a same-content-in-two-places model. — [Heptabase wiki: Nested whiteboard](https://wiki.heptabase.com/nested-whiteboard)
- Capacities' release notes describe embed layouts (items displayed as an embed with a toggle); no block-level substitute/roll-up feature was found. — [Capacities release 25](https://capacities.io/whats-new/release-25)

### Inferences
- All sourced mechanisms are *identity-preserving* (mirror, embed, synced block) or *one-shot copy* (Roam replace-with-text). ThinkPad's resolve is *reductive substitution*: the child must shrink to one paragraph, that paragraph replaces a span, and the derivation is kept out of the text. I found no shipped equivalent. The closest conceptual relatives are outside note apps: a merged pull request / squash-merge (work on a branch collapses to one change on the parent), and a collapsed toggle holding the working-out under a summary line. Both are analogies, not sourced feature matches.
- Borrowable: (a) Workflowy's "where are all the mirrors" menu maps onto "show the archived working-out behind this paragraph" (a provenance affordance on a resolved span); (b) Roam's four-way choice when flattening a reference (embed / text / alias / text+alias) suggests offering more than one resolve outcome, e.g. replace the span, or keep the original and append the result; (c) Logseq's reference counter suggests a small marker on a parent paragraph showing it has a history of N drills; (d) Roam's embed-with-path suggests showing the breadcrumb of origin when viewing an archived sub-pad out of context.
- Staging several results and applying them together as a numbered draft has a clear analogue only in version-control practice (batching commits into a release), not in any sourced note tool.
- Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Notion database "rollup" properties and Tana fields/search nodes aggregate values from related records into a parent (counts, sums, percent complete), which is roll-up of *metadata*, not of prose; Obsidian transcludes with `![[note#^block]]`. None replaces parent text with child output.

### Gaps
- Official Roam docs for "replace with text" / "apply children" wording were not found; the cited description is from a forum thread and may predate current menus.
- Tana fields, Notion rollups, Obsidian block embeds, Bike and Scrintal could not be checked against primary docs this session.
- No evidence found of any app with "child result replaces parent span, derivation archived". I state this as "not found", not "does not exist".

## Free-write then refine in separate phases; freezing a draft; versions and snapshots

### Takeaway
Many apps enforce the *drafting* half (keep typing or lose it) and several offer a distinct *revision* mode or manual snapshots, but none found freezes a draft read-only and then permits only structured revision operations. Versioning in writer-facing tools is manual and named (Scrivener snapshots, the defunct Draft's "mark draft"), and the one web app built around draft-by-draft versioning closed in 2022.

### Cited Findings
- The Most Dangerous Writing App: free web app that deletes all progress if the user stops typing for five seconds; acquired by Squibler in April 2019 with the original still freely accessible; used in free-writing contests and recommended by NaNoWriMo. — [Wikipedia: The Most Dangerous Writing App](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Most_Dangerous_Writing_App); [TechCrunch 2022 on Squibler](https://techcrunch.com/2022/12/25/the-most-dangerous-writing-app)
- Flowstate: the earlier paid Mac/iOS app it was modelled on, with near-identical behaviour (text is deleted when you stop); listed at $15 Mac / $10 iOS in 2016. — [Tech Times 2016](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/128774/20160128/flowstate-writing-app-tunes-distractions-out-deletes-everything-when-users.htm); [Quartz 2018](https://qz.com/work/1390626/a-productivity-app-with-a-new-twist-threats)
- A LessWrong review exists of The Most Dangerous Writing App as a thinking tool (user-sentiment source; content not read in full). — [LessWrong review](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/5cJq8wGw2WACm9gHR/review-the-most-dangerous-writing-app)
- Draft (Nathan Kontny, draftin.com): pitched in 2013 as version control for writing, with "clean saves of drafts instead of the usual autosave jumble" and a manual "mark draft" button. — [TechCrunch 2013](https://techcrunch.com/2013/03/12/draft-is-the-best-word-processor-you-can-write-with-today); [Chronicle ProfHacker](https://www.chronicle.com/blogs/profhacker/draft-for-collaborative-writing)
- Draft is discontinued: Beeminder's help page says Draft closed permanently at the end of 2022 (new sign-ups had been disabled earlier) and Beeminder removed its integration. — [Beeminder help: Draft](https://help.beeminder.com/article/121-draft)
- Scrivener snapshots: a manual per-document snapshot; "Compare" highlights changes between a snapshot and current text, or between two selected snapshots, at three selectable levels of granularity. — [Well-Storied: Scrivener snapshots](https://www.well-storied.com/blog/how-to-review-old-drafts-with-scrivener-snapshots); [Literature & Latte forum: Snapshot / Compare](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/snapshot-compare-how-does-it-work-technically/36539)
- Scrivener user friction: users ask for "version history" and for comparing separate documents, which snapshot-compare does not do without workarounds; a user requested a "REWRITE MODE" where one rewrites a scene alongside the old one. This shows demand for old-version-visible-while-rewriting. — [L&L forum: Version history?](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/version-history/124722); [L&L forum: File comparison?](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/file-comparison/131335); [L&L forum: rewrite mode request](https://forum.literatureandlatte.com/t/version-2-9-9-16-beta-1183694-64-bit-feature-request/94426)
- Scrivener has a "Revision Mode" in which new text is typed in a per-revision colour. — [Literature & Latte blog: Revision Mode](https://www.literatureandlatte.com/blog/how-to-use-revision-mode-to-edit-your-scrivener-projects)
- Ulysses revision mode (Mac in v20, iPhone/iPad in v21, 2020): hides unnecessary features, dims the editor theme, highlights annotations and suggestions, and aggregates all annotations and comments from the sheet into one place. This is an explicit separate "revise" phase driven by the writer's own annotations. Sources are 2020-2021. — [MacRumors forum on Ulysses 21](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/ulysses-21-brings-advanced-grammar-and-style-check-to-iphone-and-ipad.2258041); [MacRumors on Ulysses 23](https://www.macrumors.com/2021/08/03/ulysses-23-blog-publishing-session-history/)
- iA Writer: Focus Mode dims everything but the current sentence or paragraph; Syntax Highlight marks parts of speech to expose style; "Authorship" marks text as human, AI or reference and dims non-own text, toggled from the Focus menu (Shift-Cmd-A), with annotations stored at the end of the file; Authorship was reported coming to Windows. iA Writer keeps a documented version history. — [iA Writer support: Authorship](https://ia.net/writer/support/editor/authorship); [iA Writer support: Version history](https://ia.net/writer/support/help/version-history); [Thurrott on Windows Authorship](https://www.thurrott.com/windows/windows-11/334306/ia-writer-for-windows-is-getting-authorship-capabilities)
- Lex (AI-assisted writing app): a single low-authority source reports founder Nathan Baschez saying in October 2026 that Lex is winding down by the end of the year; treat as unconfirmed. Earlier primary sources show it was spun out of Every and raised a $2.75M seed. — [joaoqueiros.com summary (low authority)](https://www.ai.joaoqueiros.com/blog/lex-ai-writing-winding-down-nathan-baschez-chatgpt); [Every: spinning out Lex](https://every.to/p/we-re-spinning-out-lex); [TechCrunch 2023](https://techcrunch.com/2023/08/23/lex-ai-writing-tool-seed-round/amp/)
- Ink & Switch has published research notes on version control for prose ("Writing prose", "Universal version control", 2024 notebook) and the earlier "Upwelling" project; pages could not be opened, so contents are not summarised here. — [Ink & Switch Patchwork notebook 02](https://www.inkandswitch.com/patchwork/notebook/02); [Upwelling](https://inkandswitch.com/upwelling/)

### Inferences
- The market splits the two phases across *different apps or modes*: "danger" apps enforce forward motion while drafting; Ulysses/Scrivener offer an opt-in revision mode. No sourced tool couples them with a one-way door. ThinkPad's stamp (freeze + enter Reflect) is such a door, and appears novel as a combination.
- The danger apps enforce separation with a *penalty* (deletion). ThinkPad enforces it *structurally* (the draft becomes immutable; change only via drill/resolve). The structural route avoids the main risk the penalty model carries, which is losing work; one Flowstate user reported losing files after an update (search-summary claim from an App Store review; weak evidence).
- Borrowable: Ulysses' revision-mode pattern of dimming the body and listing every annotation in one panel is a direct model for a Reflect-phase "open items" list; Scrivener's two-snapshot compare with selectable granularity (three levels) is a model for showing draft v(n) against v(n+1); Scrivener's colour-per-revision idea could mark which spans changed in which numbered draft; iA's Authorship store-annotations-at-end-of-file approach is a plain-text-friendly way to persist paragraph tags without polluting the prose.
- The Scrivener "rewrite alongside the old scene" request and the lack of whole-document version history are evidence of unmet demand for exactly what numbered draft versions plus peek-at-parent provide.
- Draft's closure (2022) and Lex's reported wind-down suggest standalone hosted writing apps are fragile; a local-first tool is insulated from that failure mode.

### Gaps
- 750 Words: no usable results (pricing, status, whether it blocks editing). Background knowledge only: it is a private daily free-writing site with a word target and post-hoc stats; unverified this session.
- Typora, and Ulysses' current (2026) feature set, pricing and status, were not confirmed; Ulysses sources are 2020-2021.
- Whether Scrivener snapshot compare is still macOS-only is unclear; the claim surfaced from a probably dated source.
- No app found that freezes a draft and restricts edits to structured operations. Not found, rather than proven absent.
- Ink & Switch's findings on prose versioning could not be read (host unreachable); a report writer with access should consult the two notebook pages above, as they are likely the most relevant prior research on how writers think about drafts.

## Lightweight annotation of your own text and how it becomes follow-up work

### Takeaway
Annotation-then-process workflows are well established for *other people's* text (Readwise highlights, progressive summarization, Zettelkasten literature notes) and the recurring criticism is that marking is cheap, feels like progress, and produces a backlog that is never processed. Tagging one's *own* frozen draft with a small fixed vocabulary of intents (Question / Expand / Flag) that each open a work unit was not found in the sourced tools; the nearest is Ulysses gathering the writer's own annotations into a revision list.

### Cited Findings
- Progressive summarization (Tiago Forte) is layered highlighting of saved notes; Forte's own guidelines say it is "absolutely NOT the goal to stuff every single note through all the layers". — [Forte Labs: Progressive Summarization](https://fortelabs.com/blog/progressive-summarization/); [Every: Progressive Summarization III](https://every.to/praxis/progressive-summarization-iii-guidelines-538093)
- Criticism (Nick Milo): the method can lead to "over-collecting, over-summarization and under thinking"; he invokes the Collector's Fallacy, that collecting "feels like progress but it's not". — [Nick Milo on Medium](https://medium.com/@nickmilo22/why-progressive-summarization-must-die-c2635d1f79f1); a note collecting such critiques: [Lesley Lai: criticism of progressive summarization](https://notes.lesleylai.info/Concepts/criticism-of-progressive-summarization)
- Zettelkasten practice: at least one forum user says they do not "transform" fleeting notes into permanent notes at all; fleeting notes serve only as prompts to write proper notes from scratch. This matches ThinkPad's choice that a drilled paragraph opens a blank page rather than being edited in place. — [Zettelkasten Forum comment](https://forum.zettelkasten.de/discussion/comment/7509)
- A personal account of abandoning the method is titled "Returning from the Zettelkasten abyss" (sentiment source; content not read in full). — [Medium: Returning from the Zettelkasten abyss](https://medium.com/@brickbarnblog/returning-from-the-zettelkasten-abyss-ceca0b4b318e)
- General PKM critique: "the backlog grows faster than capacity to address it, eventually causing paralysis" (from a critique document about LLM-integrated PKM, a general observation rather than a study). — [rjroy: critique results](https://rjroy.github.io/ideas/llm-integrated-pkm/research/critique-results)
- Readwise: Reader highlights sync to Readwise and on to note apps; the official Obsidian plugin can sync new highlights automatically, with a customisable Markdown template; i.e. highlights are exported as raw material, and the "processing" step is left to the user in another app. — [Readwise docs: Obsidian export](https://docs.readwise.io/readwise/docs/exporting-highlights/obsidian); [Readwise docs: Reader exporting FAQ](https://docs.readwise.io/reader/docs/faqs/exporting)
- Ulysses revision mode collects all of the writer's own annotations and comments on a sheet into "a single hub for all potential revisions". — [MacRumors forum on Ulysses 21](https://forums.macrumors.com/threads/ulysses-21-brings-advanced-grammar-and-style-check-to-iphone-and-ipad.2258041)

### Inferences
- The known failure mode of annotate-then-process systems is an unbounded, guilt-inducing backlog. ThinkPad has two structural defences the sourced tools lack: the annotated corpus is one bounded draft (not an ever-growing library), and the open-item gate forces each tag to a decision before the level can close. This is worth stating as a design rationale, since it answers the Milo/Collector's-Fallacy critique directly.
- The same critique is a warning: if tagging in Reflect is too cheap, users may over-tag and then face a wall of obligations at the gate. Mitigations suggested by the sources: Forte's "not every note through every layer" (make "park" and "accept as caveat" first-class and guilt-free), and a visible count of open items while tagging.
- The Zettelkasten "fleeting note is a prompt, not a draft to edit" practice supports the blank sub-pad: rewriting from a prompt rather than polishing the original text.
- Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Andy Matuschak's notes describe a "writing inbox" for transient notes and evergreen notes that are refined over time; Maggie Appleton's digital-garden essays describe seedling/budding/evergreen maturity labels. Both are status-labelling schemes for own notes that could inform how ThinkPad labels a level (open / converged / resolved). Not verified here because the host was unreachable.

### Gaps
- No source found for an app where a fixed set of intent tags on one's own paragraphs each spawn a work unit. Not found, not proven absent.
- Matuschak, Appleton and Gordon Brander primary texts were not retrieved; nothing from them is cited.
- No quantitative data found on how often highlights or fleeting notes are ever processed.

## Convergence or completeness gates

### Takeaway
A hard gate exists in Org-mode (a parent cannot be marked DONE while it has unfinished TODO children) and soft, automatic roll-ups exist in issue trackers (Linear auto-closes a parent when all sub-issues are done), but nothing found gates *writing* on open questions or offers ThinkPad's four dispositions (address, accept as caveat, lift to a higher level, park). The size condition "converged to a single paragraph" has no sourced equivalent at all.

### Cited Findings
- Org-mode: with `org-enforce-todo-dependencies` enabled, "Org blocks entries from changing state to DONE while they have TODO children that are not DONE". The `ORDERED` property additionally forces children to be done in sequence; the `NOBLOCKING` property exempts an entry from blocking; `org-enforce-todo-checkbox-dependencies` applies the same rule to checkboxes. — [Org manual: TODO dependencies](https://www.gnu.org/software/emacs/manual/html_node/org/TODO-dependencies.html)
- User friction with the Org gate: a 2011 mailing-list thread reports a Lisp error "TODO state change from TODO to DONE blocked" that went away only when the variable was set to nil; i.e. when the gate fires unexpectedly, users turn it off. — [emacs-orgmode list, March 2011](https://lists.gnu.org/r/emacs-orgmode/2011-03/msg00240.html)
- Linear (Sept 2024): two optional automations: parent auto-close ("when all sub-issues are marked as done, the parent issue will also be marked as complete automatically") and sub-issue auto-close (completing the parent completes all remaining sub-issues). These are roll-ups, not blocks. — [Linear changelog 2024-09-06](https://linear.app/changelog/2024-09-06-auto-close-parent-and-sub-issues)
- GitHub: sub-issues are a native feature with a REST API (view, add, remove, reprioritise); no source found that GitHub natively prevents closing a parent with open sub-issues; closing behaviour in either direction is done with Actions workflows or the GitHub Next "Sub-Issue Closer" agentic workflow, which closes parents whose sub-issues are all complete (daily by default). — [GitHub REST docs: sub-issues](https://docs.github.com/en/rest/issues/sub-issues); [GitHub changelog 2024-12-12](https://github.blog/changelog/2024-12-12-github-issues-projects-close-issue-as-a-duplicate-rest-api-for-sub-issues-and-more/); [GitHub Next: sub-issue closer](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/githubnext/agentics/main/docs/sub-issue-closer.md)
- Jira: closing a parent when all sub-tasks (and linked issues) are closed is achieved with automation rules, per Atlassian's own knowledge-base articles; a native block was not confirmed. — [Atlassian KB: close parent when sub-tasks closed](https://support.atlassian.com/automation/kb/close-the-parent-issue-when-all-its-sub-tasks-are-closed/); [Atlassian KB: parent closed when sub-tasks and linked issues close](https://confluence.atlassian.com/jirakb/jira-automation-parent-issue-should-be-closed-when-all-sub-tasks-and-linked-issue-close-1442156205.html)

### Inferences
- Org-mode is the only sourced tool with a true block, and its design carries three lessons: the gate is opt-in (a variable); there is a per-node escape hatch (`NOBLOCKING`); and blocked items are a known source of confusion when the reason is not shown. ThinkPad's dispositions (caveat, lift, park) are richer escape hatches than `NOBLOCKING` because each records *why* the item is not being resolved, and "lift to a higher level" has no sourced analogue (the nearest idea is re-parenting a sub-issue).
- Borrowable: from Org, make the block message name the specific undecided items and offer the dispositions inline (avoid the bare "blocked" error that made a user disable the feature); from Linear, the complementary automation: when the last open item at a level is decided, offer to resolve the level rather than requiring a separate action; from Org's `ORDERED`, an optional "resolve in order" constraint is probably not worth copying for prose.
- Issue trackers treat completeness as a binary on children. ThinkPad's gate is on *decisions about open moves*, plus a *convergence* test on the text itself. The second half (text must shrink to one paragraph before it can be folded back) appears to be unique among everything surveyed.
- Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Workflowy and Dynalist let a parent be completed regardless of open children; Notion has a database "dependencies" (blocking / blocked by) feature and sub-items, and Tana supports checkbox fields and searches for undone children; none of these blocks completion as far as I know. Unverified.

### Gaps
- Notion dependencies/sub-items and Tana were not verified against primary docs.
- Whether Jira workflow validators/conditions can natively block a parent transition was left unverified by the search.
- No writing or thinking tool with any kind of completion gate was found.

## Per-app status, pricing, platform and local-first posture (as of Nov 2026)

### Takeaway
Of the comparable apps, the genuinely local-first ones with plain files are Org-mode, Obsidian, Logseq (file-based "OG", now frozen) and Scrivener/iA Writer; most outliner-with-zoom tools (Workflowy, Roam, Tana, Notion, Dynalist) are cloud services. Several are in flux in 2026: Logseq split into a frozen Markdown edition and a beta database edition, Muse is being renamed Allume, Dynalist is in long-term maintenance, Draft is closed, and Lex is reportedly winding down.

### Inferences
- For a local-first, single-user, human-writes-everything tool, the stable reference points are Org-mode, Obsidian, iA Writer and Scrivener; the cloud outliners supply the interaction patterns (zoom, mirrors) but not the data model.
- 2026 turbulence (Logseq's file-to-database move and frozen OG edition, Dynalist dormancy, Muse rename, Draft and Lex ending) is a recurring user anxiety in this category; durability of local data and exportable plain text are selling points worth emphasising.
- AI pivots: iA Writer's response to AI is notable because it is *anti-generation* (Authorship marks and dims text you did not write), which aligns with Mindstream's "the human does all the writing" stance; Tana, Notion, Heptabase and Lex have leaned toward AI features (background knowledge for Tana/Notion/Heptabase; not sourced in detail this session).
- Background knowledge, not sourced this session: Workflowy is cloud with a free tier and a paid Pro plan; Notion is cloud freemium; Scrivener is a one-time purchase on Mac/Windows/iOS storing local project files; Ulysses is a subscription for Apple platforms only; Bike (Hog Bay Software) is a paid native Mac outliner with local files; Obsidian is free with paid Sync/Publish add-ons; 750 Words is a low-cost web subscription. Verify before publishing.
